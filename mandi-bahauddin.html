Mandi Bahauddin

Doab market town · code 0546 · district since 1993

Two rivers hold this file in place. The Jhelum runs about twelve kilometres north of the headquarters. The Chenab lies farther south. Between them sits a grain market that outgrew the village Pindi Bahauddin, took the saint’s name as a suffix, and kept mandi as the prefix because wheat still sets the morning. Elevation is a little over two hundred metres. The old marketing joke “city of lions” is a slogan. The working fact is simpler: this is a canal-and-barrage district that sells grain, sends soldiers and cricketers out, and reads Punjabi at home.

The 2023 count for the whole district is 1,829,486 residents on 2,673 km². Households number 285,989. Towns hold about 346,000 people; the rest remain rural. Men and women are almost level on the sheet (917,125 and 912,222). Among those aged ten and older, 70.27 percent can read — 74.89 percent of men, 65.70 percent of women. That last figure is high for rural Punjab and is the quiet achievement here. Punjabi is the first language of about 1.70 million people. Urdu follows at roughly 84,000. Pashto is a smaller migrant row.

Mandi Bahauddin: How the market became a headquarters

In 1506 a Gondal chief remembered as Bahauddin settled Pindi Bahauddin after leaving Pindi Shah Jahanian. The modern grid grew in the early twentieth century around a chak where Sikh, Hindu and Muslim traders shared the same mandi floor. Partition emptied one side of that floor and filled it from the other. Separate district status arrived on 1 July 1993, when Mian Manzoor Wattoo’s Punjab government cut the file away from Gujrat. Phalia and Malakwal became the other two tehsil seats.

Local memory also keeps two battle names. Near Mong, schoolbooks place Alexander’s fight with Porus in 326 BCE. Treat that as a taught story. The fight that sits on a surveyed field is Chillianwala, January 1849 — Second Anglo-Sikh War.

Mandi Bahauddin: Water that pays the rent

Rasul Barrage on the Jhelum and Qadirabad Barrage on the Chenab turned Gondal Bar into irrigated squares. Wheat first, then cane, fodder, citrus, livestock. The barrage, not the cloud, writes the yield.

Three tehsils of Mandi Bahauddin District (List)

Headquarters tehsil — 759 km², 764,532 people, reading rate 72.69 percent. City count 232,361.

Phalia — 1,155 km², 635,651 people, reading rate 70.11 percent. Qadirabad and the Chenab-side works.

Malakwal — 759 km², 429,303 people, reading rate 66.28 percent. Railway town. Folk singers travelled farther than the grain.

Mandi Bahauddin: Voices that left the mandi

Amrita Pritam (1919–2005) was born Amrit Kaur here and became the century’s most widely read woman in Punjabi letters. Daim Iqbal Daim wrote Punjabi narrative verse from this soil. Ahmad Salim was born in Miana Gondal in 1945. Shaukat Ali and Maratab Ali carried Malakwal onto radio. Hasan Ali is the fast-bowling name. Naved Arif played a short international spell. Haroon Yousaf captained Pakistan at football. Air Chief Marshal Pervaiz Mehdi Qureshi is the service name.

Assembly houses: Bosal, Chan, Gondal, Rizvi, Tarar, Ranjha. Nasir Iqbal Bosal, Imdad Ullah Bosal, Nadeem Afzal Chan, Nazar Muhammad Gondal, Mumtaz Ahmed Tarar, Muhammad Tariq Tarar, Basma Riaz Choudhry, Khalid Mahmood Ranjha. Hasan Askari Rizvi later served a caretaker term as Punjab chief minister.

Mandi Bahauddin: What a student actually uses

Colleges in the headquarters, Phalia and Malakwal. Medicine and engineering still mean Gujrat, Jhelum or Lahore. DHQ first; heavier cases follow the same roads.
