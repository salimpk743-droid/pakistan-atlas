Attock District

Rawalpindi Division · headquarters Attock City · six tehsils on the Indus, Soan and Haro · PBS 2023

Attock is Punjab’s north-west corner, the place where the Grand Trunk Road and the railway still have to negotiate the Indus. The fort on the left bank is why the name travelled; the district behind it is larger and quieter than the postcard. Campbellpur was the colonial town name. Attock City is the headquarters people use now. This page is not a reprint of the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa valley notes on this atlas. The land here is Potohar plateau, Kala Chitta ridge, and rainfed wheat — not a single gorge with one jeep road.

The 2023 sheet, read slowly

Public 2023 census tables for Attock District list about 2.17 million people (the detailed household table is commonly cited as 2,170,423 in 353,973 households). A shorter box in some summaries prints 2,133,005. This atlas keeps both figures in view and does not invent a third. Area is 6,857 km². Density is about 316 people per km². Roughly seven in ten residents are rural (about 1.55 million); about 624,000 are counted urban.

Literacy for ages 10 and above is 70.22 percent — 79.69 percent for men, 60.66 percent for women. That female figure is the education fact that matters more than a list of campus names. Under-tens are a little over one-fifth of the surveyed population. Phone code 057. Postal code often given as 43600. Official district site: attock.punjab.gov.pk.

Six tehsils, not one market town

The district is not “Attock City plus countryside.” It is six tehsils with different densities and different problems:

Attock tehsil — about 1,002 km² and 516,277 people (2023). Headquarters belt. Literacy near 75 percent. Kamra and the river villages sit in this column.

Hazro — smallest land of the six (about 348 km²) and the densest, about 386,544 people. Literacy near 66 percent. Chhachh plain, closer to the river gardens than to the dry south.

Hassan Abdal — about 350 km² and 253,670 people. GT Road town. Gurdwara Panja Sahib is the name travellers already know. Literacy printed with the district average in some tehsil rows.

Fateh Jang — about 1,249 km² and 374,726 people. Literacy near 67 percent. Plateau and broken ground between the Haro and the Soan catchments.

Pindi Gheb — about 1,865 km² and 308,878 people. Wide, drier west. Literacy near 70 percent.

Jand — largest land, about 2,043 km², 330,328 people, lowest density. Literacy near 72 percent. This is the tehsil where distance, not a bazaar crowd, decides whether a teacher arrives.

Union-council counts published with the district sit in the low seventies. Village counts differ by source (a few hundred on the district profile; a much higher figure on some trade notes). This page does not pick a village total and pretend it is a census cell.

Rivers, ridge and rainfed ground

Three water names organise the map: the Indus on the west and north-west, the Haro coming off the Hazara side, and the Soan cutting the plateau. Between them the Kala Chitta range splits the northern tehsils from the drier south. Most farmland here is barani — rainfed — not canal-colony square fields. Wheat and oilseed are the ordinary crops. Livestock still pays a large share of household work. Cement limestone, Attock’s older oil field and refinery story, and the Pakistan Aeronautical Complex at Kamra are the industrial names that sit beside that farm calendar. They employ some families. They do not rewrite Jand’s rain year.

Who speaks what

The 2023 mother-tongue table is mixed in a way central Punjab is not. Punjabi (including the local Potohari speech) is the largest row — on the order of 1.39 million speakers in the published language table. Pashto is the next large row (about 332,000). Hindko is close behind (about 307,000). Urdu is a smaller urban and school row. That mix is geography: the Indus crossing, the GT Road, and older settlements from both the Peshawar side and the Potohar side. It is not a ranking of communities.

Classrooms and wards

Public tallies put well over a thousand government schools in the district, with girls’ schools a little over half of that stock in some published lists, and public enrolment in the low hundreds of thousands. Degree options inside the district include government graduate and women’s colleges in Attock, Fateh Jang, Pindi Gheb, Jand, Hazro and Hassan Abdal; a University of Education campus; COMSATS Attock; and the Air University aerospace campus tied to Kamra. Cadet College Hasan Abdal is the boarding name many families outside the district already know. Students still leave for Rawalpindi, Islamabad and Taxila for specialised degrees. That is ordinary, not a failure of one tehsil.

The health map is a DHQ in Attock City (often listed as the IYB District Hospital) and five tehsil headquarters hospitals — Hazro, Hassan Abdal, Fateh Jang, Jand and Pindi Gheb — plus RHCs and a long string of BHUs. Serious cases still travel toward Rawalpindi teaching hospitals. Rural teacher shortages, reported again in 2026, are the education problem that sits next to those campus names: urban schools can show a surplus on paper while a multi-grade village school waits for a posting.

What this page will not do

It will not sell Attock Fort as the whole district. It will not copy the same three headings used on the Hazara pages of this atlas. It will not treat Kamra or Panja Sahib as a substitute for literacy and rain. Named places students can actually look up include Attock City, Kamra, Hazro, Hassan Abdal, Fateh Jang, Pindi Gheb, Jand, Ghorghushti, and the river villages under the fort.
