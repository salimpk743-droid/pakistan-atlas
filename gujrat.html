Gujrat District

Gujrat Division headquarters · between Chenab and Jhelum · phone 053

Gujrat District: Quick Answers

Gujrat District is one of the 41 districts of Punjab, with its headquarters at Gujrat. It has 3 tehsils: Gujrat, Kharian and Sarai Alamgir. The 2023 census counted 3,219,375 people on 3,192 km², and 81.37% of residents aged 10 and over were literate.

Gujrat sits in the narrow doab between the Chenab and the Jhelum. Gujranwala is across the Chenab. Jhelum district is across the Jhelum. Bhimber and Mirpur are over the Line of Control to the north. That geography made a market town. What filled the town later was not another textile mill belt. It was fans, fired clay, carved wood, and a road out of Kharian that still sends more young men to Europe than to Lahore.

PBS 2023: 3,219,375 people in about 489,000 households on 3,192 km². Density about 1,007 per km². Urban about 1.32 million (41 percent). Literacy at age ten and above is 81.37 percent — 84.81 percent for men, 77.95 percent for women. Among Punjab districts that is a high sheet. Gujrat tehsil itself prints about 82.5 percent. Under-tens: about 718,000. Official site: gujrat.punjab.gov.pk. The district profile still lists three revenue tehsils, 117 union councils and a little over a thousand villages.

Gujrat, Kharian, Sarai Alamgir

Gujrat tehsil — 1,463 km², 1,746,173 people, literacy about 82.5 percent. Headquarters, University of Gujrat, the fan and ceramic cluster, Jalalpur Jattan, Kunjah. City tehsil in all but name.

Kharian — 1,154 km², 1,174,935 people, literacy about 79.7 percent. Cantonment, GT Road, the villages that live on remittances. If you meet a Pakistani bus driver in Barcelona or a shopkeeper in Oslo who says he is from “Gujrat,” there is a fair chance the village is in this tehsil.

Sarai Alamgir — 575 km², 298,267 people, literacy about 81.6 percent. Smallest tehsil. Jhelum-side, the old sarai on the imperial road, now a municipal town with its own THQ.

Jalalpur Jattan and Kunjah appear on newer local lists as extra tehsil or town units. On the 2023 census sheet used here they still sit inside Gujrat tehsil. Jalalpur is the fan town. Kunjah is the older qasba.

What the workshops actually make

The district profile counts, in round official numbers: about 362 fan units, 137 pottery / ceramic units, 784 furniture workshops, a handful of textile houses and a long tail of other small industry — some 1,500 listed units in all. Those numbers move. The pattern does not.

Electric fans from Gujrat and Jalalpur Jattan are in half the rooms of Pakistan. The work is metal stamping, winding, paint and a brand name that may or may not be printed on the blade. Ceramics — tableware, sanitary ware, tiles — use local clay skill and imported glaze chemistry. Furniture is the third street: carved and polished wood that leaves for Karachi, the Gulf and the houses overseas money builds on the Kharian road. Shoes and a little textile sit on the side. Farming is still wheat, rice and fodder on more than half a million cultivated acres, part canal, part barani.

This is not Faisalabad’s loom economy and not Gujranwala’s scrap-and-fitting economy. It is a craft-plus-assembly district with a second payroll that never appears in the factory register: the euro and the pound that land in Kharian banks.

The road that leaves Kharian

Overseas work is the other industry. Families in Kharian tehsil have been sending sons to the Gulf, then to Italy, Spain, Greece, Norway and the UK, for two generations. Houses go up with Italian tiles. Schools fill because a father in Barcelona pays the fee. The risk is obvious and already on the district’s own “issues” line: if the local fan shop and the ceramic kiln do not keep the next brother, the district becomes a waiting room for a visa. Remittance built the ring road housing. It does not automatically train a glaze technician.

People this district is known for

An atlas page is not a birthday card. The names below are the ones students already look up. They are not the whole district.

Major Raja Aziz Bhatti, Nishan-e-Haider, is the military name. He was born in Hong Kong to a family whose village is Ladian, a few miles from Gujrat city. He was killed on the Lahore front in September 1965. He is buried in that ancestral courtyard. The public teaching hospital in Gujrat carries his name: Aziz Bhatti Shaheed Hospital / DHQ.

The Chaudhry house of Gujrat is the political name. Chaudhry Zahoor Elahi was the elder who entered national politics and was killed in 1981. From that house came Chaudhry Shujaat Hussain, briefly prime minister of Pakistan, and Chaudhry Parvez Elahi, twice chief minister of Punjab and once deputy prime minister. Moonis Elahi and other children of that generation still contest the Gujrat seats. Zahoor Elahi House and Zahoor Elahi Stadium are city landmarks whether a reader likes the family or not. The industrial side of the same clan — textile after Partition — is part of why the politics and the workshops grew together.

Aitzaz Ahsan, the barrister and former law minister, is a Gujrat name in a different register: courtrooms and the Senate more than the district council.

Sir Anwar Pervez, who built Bestway in Britain, is the overseas-business name most often tied to the Kharian side of this district. He is the public example of the same road the anonymous bus drivers use.

Wrestlers and club players exist here as they do in Gujranwala. They do not need a false hall of fame. The hospital, the university, the fan shed and the Kharian passport office are the institutions that outlast a headline.

University town on a small doab

University of Gujrat is the public campus the district profile means when it says “one university.” Government colleges — boys’, girls’ and commerce — sit in the city, Kharian, Sarai Alamgir, Jalalpur Jattan and Kunjah. About 1,470 government schools, a majority of them girls’ schools on the official count. Literacy at 81 percent is the result of that stock plus the remittance fee. Students still leave for Lahore for medicine and engineering seats the local campuses cannot hold.

Aziz Bhatti hospital and the tehsil line

Aziz Bhatti Shaheed Teaching Hospital / DHQ is the main public hospital. The profile lists four THQ hospitals, nine RHCs and about ninety BHUs. Kharian and Sarai Alamgir take the cantonment and the Jhelum-side load. Private houses follow the GT Road. Specialist cases still move to Lahore or Rawalpindi when the teaching ward is full.

Speech

Punjabi is the mother tongue of about 93 percent on the 2023 table. Urdu about 3.5 percent. Pashto about 2 percent — a migrant and labour row, not a hill district. This is Chenab-Jhelum Punjabi, closer to Gujranwala than to Jhelum city in daily speech.

Names on the ground

Gujrat city, Jalalpur Jattan, Kunjah, Kharian, Kharian Cantonment, Sarai Alamgir, Ladian, the GT Road villages that live on a foreign roster.

Gujrat is a small doab that learned to stamp a fan, fire a plate and send a son abroad. The 2023 sheet is 3.22 million people and a literacy row most of southern Punjab would envy. The open question is the same one the district already writes for itself: whether the kiln and the furniture shop still have work when the next generation would rather wait for a European appointment than take a shift in Jalalpur Jattan.

Gujrat District: FAQ

How many tehsils are in Gujrat District?

Gujrat District has 3 tehsils: Gujrat, Kharian and Sarai Alamgir.

What is the population of Gujrat District?

The 2023 census counted 3,219,375 people in Gujrat District, up from 2,756,289 in 2017 (annual growth 2.63%). 41.13% of the population lived in urban areas.

What is the area of Gujrat District?

Gujrat District covers 3,192 km² according to the 2023 census, a density of 1,008.58 people per km².

What is the literacy rate of Gujrat District?

81.37% of people aged 10 and over were literate in the 2023 census (male 84.81%, female 77.95%).

Which languages are spoken in Gujrat District?

The most common mother tongue in the 2023 census was Punjabi (93.47% of the 3,216,000 people in the mother-tongue table), followed by Urdu (3.48%), Pashto (2.16%).

Which province is Gujrat District in?

Gujrat District is in Punjab. Its headquarters is Gujrat.
