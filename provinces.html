Provinces & territories of Pakistan

Four provinces, the federal capital territory and two further territories. Population and area follow the 2023 census (PBS; for Gilgit-Baltistan as published by its Planning & Development Department) and, for Azad Kashmir, the 2017 census published by the AJ&K Bureau of Statistics. See also the original Provinces of Pakistan page.

Punjab پنجاب

Province Capital: Lahore · Area 205,345 km² · Population 127,688,922 (2023 census)

Punjab is a province of Pakistan with its capital at Lahore. The 2023 census counted 127,688,922 people (52.87% of Pakistan) on 205,345 km², and 66.25% of residents aged 10 and over were literate.

Culture

Punjab is the cultural heartland of Sufi poetry, Qawwali, Bhangra and harvest festivals such as Basant. Major languages are Punjabi and Saraiki. Historic cities include Lahore, Multan and Taxila.

Public issues

Urban crowding in Lahore and Faisalabad, smog, groundwater stress, and the need to expand technical education in southern districts.

Universities & colleges

Highest number of universities and colleges in the country, including University of the Punjab, UET Lahore and UVAS.

Sindh سندھ

Province Capital: Karachi · Area 140,914 km² · Population 55,696,147 (2023 census)

Sindh is a province of Pakistan with its capital at Karachi. The 2023 census counted 55,696,147 people (23.06% of Pakistan) on 140,914 km², and 57.54% of residents aged 10 and over were literate.

Culture

Sindh is the land of Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai, the Indus civilisation sites of Mohenjo-daro, Ajrak, Sindhi topi and the annual urs at Sehwan and Bhit Shah.

Public issues

Karachi municipal services, rural waterlogging, coastal erosion and flood recovery after 2022.

Universities & colleges

University of Karachi, NED, IBA and Sindh Agriculture University Tandojam are major institutions.

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa خیبر پختونخوا

Province Capital: Peshawar · Area 101,741 km² · Population 40,856,097 (2023 census)

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is a province of Pakistan with its capital at Peshawar. The 2023 census counted 40,856,097 people (16.92% of Pakistan) on 101,741 km², and 51.09% of residents aged 10 and over were literate.

Culture

Pashtunwali hospitality, Attan dance, rabab music and historic passes of Khyber and Malakand. Former FATA agencies are now districts of KP.

Public issues

Mountain access, climate shocks, and integrating newly merged districts into the provincial service system.

Universities & colleges

University of Peshawar, UET Peshawar, AWKUM and Gomal University.

Balochistan بلوچستان

Province Capital: Quetta · Area 347,190 km² · Population 14,894,402 (2023 census)

Balochistan is a province of Pakistan with its capital at Quetta. The 2023 census counted 14,894,402 people (6.17% of Pakistan) on 347,190 km², and 42.01% of residents aged 10 and over were literate.

Culture

Largest province by area. Baloch and Brahui traditions, juniper forests of Ziarat, Makran coast and the historic Quetta valley.

Public issues

Low population density, water scarcity, school access for girls in remote tehsils, and long travel times to hospitals.

Universities & colleges

University of Balochistan and BUITEMS in Quetta are the main campuses; more district colleges are being added.

Gilgit-Baltistan گلگت بلتستان

Administrative Territory Area 72,496 km² · Population 1,709,049 (2023 census)

Gilgit-Baltistan, formerly the Northern Areas, had 1,709,049 people in the 2023 census, up from 1,492,924 in 2017, on 72,496 km². Its government notified 14 districts in 2019; official statistics are still published for 10.

Culture

Meeting point of Karakoram, Himalaya and Hindu Kush. Shina, Balti, Burushaski and Wakhi languages. Polo at Shandur and apricot culture of Hunza.

Public issues

Glacial lake floods, road closures in winter, and tourism carrying capacity.

Universities & colleges

Karakoram International University Gilgit and growing college network.

Azad Jammu and Kashmir آزاد جموں و کشمیر

Administrative Territory Capital: Muzaffarabad · Area 13,297 km² · Population 4,032,363 (2017 census)

Azad Jammu and Kashmir is administered from Muzaffarabad and has 10 districts in 3 divisions. The 2017 census counted 4,032,363 people on 13,297 km²; the AJ&K Bureau of Statistics projects 4,460,971 for 2023.

Culture

Kashmiri, Pahari and Gojri heritage. Neelum and Jhelum valleys, traditional wooden architecture and mountain crafts.

Public issues

Terrain, winter access, and earthquake-resilient construction after 2005.

Universities & colleges

University of AJK and several public colleges in each district.

All 10 districts of Azad Jammu and Kashmir →

Islamabad Capital Territory اسلام آباد

Federal Territory Capital: Islamabad · Area 906 km² · Population 2,363,863 (2023 census)

Islamabad Capital Territory is the federal capital territory of Pakistan with its capital at Islamabad. The 2023 census counted 2,363,863 people (0.98% of Pakistan) on 906 km², and 83.97% of residents aged 10 and over were literate.

Culture

Planned capital designed by Constantinos Doxiadis. Faisal Mosque, Margalla Hills and a mix of communities from every province.

Public issues

Rapid housing growth, traffic and pressure on the Margalla ecosystem.

Universities & colleges

QAU, NUST, COMSATS, IIUI and many federal colleges.
