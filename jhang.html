Jhang District

Faisalabad Division · Sial town on the Chenab · phone 047 · postcode 40100

Jhang District: Quick Answers

Jhang District is one of the 41 districts of Punjab, with its headquarters at Jhang. It has 4 tehsils: 18-Hazari, Ahmadpur Sial, Jhang and Shorkot. The 2023 census counted 3,065,639 people on 6,166 km², and 59.45% of residents aged 10 and over were literate.

Jhang is older than the district file. The Sial founded the town in the fifteenth century on the Chenab. Two rivers frame the land: the Chenab through the east and the Jhelum system on the west, with the Trimmu headworks tying the water together. Chiniot was cut away in 2009. What remains is still a large rural district — cane, wheat, cattle, shrine towns — whose first sentence in any Punjabi classroom is not a census row. It is a story. Heer was a Sial girl of this country. Ranjha came to her father’s herd from Takht Hazara. The district has to live with that fame and with a literacy row that is still only about sixty percent.

PBS 2023 tables used with the present border give about 3,065,639 people on 6,166 km². Density about 499 per km². Urban 800,926. Rural 2,264,713. Men 1,585,616, women 1,479,771. Under-tens about 837,000. Literacy at age ten and above is 59.45 percent — 69.09 percent for men and 49.14 percent for women. The female half of that sentence is the education fact. Some lists print a slightly higher headcount when Mandi Shah Jeewna is counted as a fifth tehsil; the four-tehsil sheet below adds to 3.07 million. Official site: jhang.punjab.gov.pk.

Jhang, Shorkot, Ahmadpur Sial, Athara Hazari

Jhang tehsil — 2,591 km², 1,640,676 people, literacy about 61.0 percent. Headquarters. Jhang city municipal count about 606,533 in 2023. This is the old Sial capital, the DHQ, the colleges.

Shorkot — 1,158 km², 604,763 people, literacy about 58.1 percent. Cantonment, shrine country toward Sultan Bahu, Garh Maharaja (about 42,400 people).

Ahmadpur Sial — 851 km², 487,905 people, literacy about 56.9 percent. Sial qasba, river villages, the weakest school row of the four.

Athara Hazari (18-Hazari) — 1,566 km², 332,295 people, literacy about 58.1 percent. Largest tehsil by empty land, smallest by people. Town itself about 30,000.

Mandi Shah Jeewna appears on newer lists as a fifth unit around the shrine of Shah Jeewna. On the 2023 tehsil table used here it is still folded into Jhang tehsil.

Jhang: Heer and Ranjha

The story that made Jhang famous is older than Waris Shah and younger than the river. In the version Punjab actually sings, Heer is the daughter of a Sial chief of Jhang. Ranjha — Dhido Ranjha — is a younger son from Takht Hazara on the Chenab, up-river toward what is now Mandi Bahauddin country. He leaves home after a quarrel over land and a flute. He walks the river. He takes work as a cowherd in Heer's father's herd. That is the first meeting: not a court, a cattle-pen. The love that follows is not a polite engagement. It is a public scandal in a cattle society that measured honour in girls and in buffalo. Heer's parents betroth her to Saida Khera of Rangpur. The marriage happens. Heer is sent away. Ranjha turns jogi, puts on ochre, and comes to Rangpur as a mendicant. The two meet again. Some tellings end with poison in Heer's food and Ranjha dying on her grave. Some end in a grave that opens. Waris Shah, writing in the eighteenth century from Jandiala Sher Khan in today’s Sheikhupura district, gave the tale the language that stuck. He was not a Jhang man. He did not need to be. He set the girl in Jhang and the boy on the Chenab, and every later singer has had to walk that map.

What Jhang does with the story is more interesting than a statue. Sial families still hear their own name in the first line. Schoolchildren from Ahmadpur Sial can point to the river and say the herd stood there, whether or not a particular well is the well. Qawwals and village singers still treat Heer as a text you learn by ear, not a novel you finish. The tale is used as romance, as a sermon against forced marriage, as a joke about buffalo, as a claim that this dusty district invented Punjabi love. All of those uses sit on top of a female literacy number of 49 percent. That gap is not a coincidence the story invented, and it is not a coincidence the story keeps finding new listeners. A girl who cannot read Waris Shah can still hear him. A district that prints the story on a tourist pamphlet and then under-funds the girls’ primary in Athara Hazari is doing both things at once.

Takht Hazara is not inside today’s Jhang file. Rangpur Khera is not a suburb of Jhang city. The geography of the poem spills into neighbouring districts the way cattle did. Jhang keeps the title because Heer is the Sial daughter. Visitors who come only for a photo at a marked “Heer tomb” will be shown a site; scholars will argue about which grave is which. The argument does not cancel the poem. The poem does not cancel the tehsil literacy table. An atlas page can hold both: the 500-year-old love that made the district famous, and the living towns that still marry the way Heer’s parents wanted to marry her.

Waris Shah’s Heer is also a book about land, jogis, qazis and the way a cattle economy talks. Read that way, Jhang is not a backdrop. It is the social machine the lovers were trying to walk out of. Cane fields and canal minors have replaced some of the old pasture. The Sial name is still on the politics. The flute is still on the loudspeaker at a shrine fair. That is the long paragraph the district owes anyone who only knows Jhang as a word in a wedding song.

Famous People from Jhang District: People this district is known for

Sultan Bahu (Sultan-ul-Arifeen), the Sarwari Qadiri poet-saint, was born in the Shorkot countryside in the seventeenth century. His shrine now stands near the Chenab after the river forced a reburial. The district government lists him first among local personalities. The urs still draws the division.

Shah Jeewna (Mehboob Alam) is the other shrine name. Mandi Shah Jeewna grew around that khanqah. The family has been in electoral politics for decades.

Abdus Salam, Nobel Prize in Physics 1979, was born in Jhang district (then including the tract around Santok Das). The particle physicist is the scientific name students look up. The town did not become a physics campus because of him. The name still belongs here.

Aleem Dar, first Pakistani on the ICC elite umpire panel, three-time David Shepherd Trophy, is the cricket name. He played first-class cricket before he stood in Tests.

Majeed Amjad is the Urdu poet. Muhammad Tahir-ul-Qadri, founder of Minhaj-ul-Quran, is the scholar-politician name. Syeda Abida Hussain (ambassador and federal minister) and Faisal Saleh Hayat (former interior minister, Shah Jeewna house) are the contemporary political names. Ghulam Bibi Bharwana is the woman MNA most often cited from the Bharwana Sial side. Khan Muhammad Arif Khan Rajbana Sial is the Pakistan Movement name the district administration still prints.

A page that lists saints and ministers is not a complete history of Jhang’s late-twentieth-century sectarian violence. That violence is part of the civic record. It is not a list of people to advertise.

Jhang: Cane, cattle, two rivers

This is not a mill city. Sugarcane, wheat, fodder and a large buffalo herd pay the rural majority. Trimmu and the Chenab-Jhelum junction make the irrigation possible and the flood possible. Chiniot took the furniture workshops when it left in 2009. What stayed is farm country with a shrine calendar.

Speech in Jhang District

Punjabi about 2.86 million speakers — roughly 93 percent. Urdu about 144,000. Saraiki a small row here, unlike Bhakkar. The folk belt is Punjabi; the shrine sermon may switch.

Jhang: Colleges and DHQ

Government colleges in Jhang, Shorkot and the smaller towns. A university presence has grown but most professional seats still mean Faisalabad or Lahore. DHQ Jhang is the public hospital. THQs cover Shorkot, Ahmadpur Sial and Athara Hazari. Shorkot Cantonment has its own medical line. River villages lose hours when the bund road goes under.

Jhang: Towns on this doab

Jhang city, Shorkot, Shorkot Cantonment, Ahmadpur Sial, Athara Hazari, Garh Maharaja, Mandi Shah Jeewna.

Jhang District: FAQ

How many tehsils are in Jhang District?

Jhang District has 4 tehsils: 18-Hazari, Ahmadpur Sial, Jhang and Shorkot.

What is the population of Jhang District?

The 2023 census counted 3,065,639 people in Jhang District, up from 2,743,526 in 2017 (annual growth 1.87%). 26.13% of the population lived in urban areas.

What is the area of Jhang District?

Jhang District covers 6,166 km² according to the 2023 census, a density of 497.18 people per km².

What is the literacy rate of Jhang District?

59.45% of people aged 10 and over were literate in the 2023 census (male 69.09%, female 49.14%).

Which languages are spoken in Jhang District?

The most common mother tongue in the 2023 census was Punjabi (93.33% of the 3,065,639 people in the mother-tongue table), followed by Urdu (4.71%).

Which province is Jhang District in?

Jhang District is in Punjab. Its headquarters is Jhang.
