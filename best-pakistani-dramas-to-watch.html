Best Pakistani Dramas of All Time: 25+ Must-Watch Drama Suggestions

From the old PTV dramas that emptied streets to the YouTube-era blockbusters with hundreds of millions of views, these are 27 Pakistani dramas worth your time. Each comes with verified cast and credits, a short spoiler-light review, and a link to an official upload where one exists.

Pakistani Drama Suggestions: How This List Works

Ask ten people for the best Pakistani dramas of all time and you will get ten lists, but some titles turn up on almost all of them. This guide brings those together: classic serials from Pakistan Television, the private-channel hits of the 2000s and 2010s, and the modern blockbusters that now draw global audiences on YouTube.

The first ten entries are, in order, the dramas our readers most asked us to include: Dhoop Kinare, Tanhaiyan, Ankahi, Dhuwan, Alpha Bravo Charlie, Raahein, Pyarey Afzal, Parizaad, Humsafar and Ishq Murshid. Seventeen more widely acclaimed dramas follow, making a list of 27. The numbering is a reading order, not a ranking. Below the quick list, the dramas are grouped by era, keeping the same numbers.

Credits come from the broadcasters' own channels and websites (PTV, HUM, Geo, ARY), from Dawn, Images Dawn, The Express Tribune, The News and ARY News, and IMDb was used only to cross-check them. Where a credit, character name or episode count could not be confirmed, we left it out rather than guess. View counts are from the official YouTube uploads as of 1 October 2026.

Old PTV Dramas: The Best Classic PTV Dramas (1979–1999)

The state broadcaster had the country to itself for most of these years, and these serials still define the golden age. Most are now free on PTV Digital's official YouTube channel.

1. Dhoop Kinare — 1987, PTV (Karachi)

  • First broadcast: 1987
  • Channel: PTV (Karachi)
  • Writer: Haseena Moin
  • Director: Sahira Kazmi
  • Episodes: 13 (PTV Digital upload: Episodes 1–12 and a last episode)
  • OST: No credited soundtrack confirmed by our sources
  • Where to watch: PTV Digital (YouTube) (Episode 1: about 399,000 views as of 1 October 2026)

Set largely inside a hospital, Haseena Moin's 1987 serial pairs two doctors who could hardly be less alike. Marina Khan's Dr Zoya is a young doctor who laughs easily, adores her child patients and will not be talked down to. Rahat Kazmi's Dr Ahmer Ansari is reserved, principled and slow to let anyone in. Their arguments on the ward turn, over thirteen unhurried episodes, into one of the gentlest love stories PTV ever put on air, and Sahira Kazmi's direction is happy to let a glance do the work of a speech.

Zoya is the reason people still rewatch it. Retrospectives in Dawn and The Express Tribune recall how her short hair and carefree manner made Marina Khan a style reference for young women, and Dawn has written about roads emptying when the play was on. Sajid Hasan, who played Dr Irfan, has spoken to Dawn about the part. A late development involving an old man's will (a mild spoiler) gives the final stretch some extra weight without breaking the light tone. At thirteen episodes there is no padding at all, which makes Dhoop Kinare an easy first stop for anyone curious about old PTV dramas.

2. Tanhaiyan — 1986, PTV

  • First broadcast: 1986
  • Channel: PTV
  • Writer: Haseena Moin
  • Director: Shehzad Khalil
  • Episodes: Not confirmed by our sources
  • OST: No credited soundtrack confirmed by our sources
  • Where to watch: PTV Digital (YouTube) (Episode 1: about 570,000 views as of 1 October 2026)

Two sisters lose their parents in a car accident, lose the family house to their father's debts and move in with their aunt Aani in Karachi. That is a bleak opening for what turns out to be one of the warmest comedies of the 1980s. Shehnaz Sheikh plays Zara, the elder sister, who decides the way back to her father's house runs through business rather than marriage, and who pays for that single-mindedness with the people closest to her.

Marina Khan made her television debut here as Sanya, the mischievous younger sister, and her running battle with Behroze Sabzwari's Qutbuddin, whom she nicknames "Qabacha", is the comic heart of the serial. Jamshed Ansari's Buqrat added the line "Zindagi ik muamma hai, jiska hal kahin kho chuka hai", written for him by Haseena Moin. Looking back in 2021, The Express Tribune argued that Moin's women (Zara, Sanya, Aani and Vida) were allowed to grow and stand on their own in a way modern serials rarely manage. Shehzad Khalil directed. Watched now, Tanhaiyan feels less like a period piece than a quiet argument that a woman's ambition can drive the plot.

3. Ankahi — 1982, PTV

  • First broadcast: 1982
  • Channel: PTV
  • Writer: Haseena Moin
  • Director: Shoaib Mansoor and Mohsin Ali
  • Episodes: 13 (PTV Digital upload)
  • OST: No credited soundtrack confirmed by our sources
  • Where to watch: PTV Digital (YouTube) (Episode 1: about 416,000 views as of 1 October 2026)

Office comedies are rare on Pakistani television, and Ankahi is the one every later attempt gets measured against. After her father's early death, Sana Murad (Shehnaz Sheikh) needs a salary, lands an office job she is not remotely qualified for and makes a string of blunders that somehow endear her to everyone. Shakeel plays the composed Taimur Ahmad, while Jawed Sheikh's confident Faraz Aafreedi falls for her. Behind the laughs sits a quieter subject the title hints at: love that goes unsaid.

The supporting cast is where the serial becomes a pleasure. Jamshed Ansari's Timmy and Behroze Sabzwari's Moby get many of the best lines, and Jawed Sheikh told Dawn that Ankahi's dialogues made him a star. Dawn has also called it one of the most watched PTV plays on the internet. In 2020 Dawn reported plans for a stage adaptation at the Arts Council in Karachi, with Amna Ilyas cast as Sana. For anyone sampling the best PTV classics, this is the lightest and quickest way in.

4. Dhuwan — 1994, PTV

  • First broadcast: 1994
  • Channel: PTV
  • Writer: Ashir Azeem
  • Director: Sajjad Ahmed
  • Episodes: Not confirmed by our sources
  • OST: No credited soundtrack confirmed by our sources
  • Where to watch: No official upload found

PTV's grittiest action drama of the 1990s began life as a novel. Ashir Azeem drafted Dhuwan during his time at the Civil Services Academy, then wrote and starred in the television version, directed by Sajjad Ahmed. The premise is simple and still gripping: five friends form a police commando unit and take on crime in and around Quetta, working as vigilantes outside the usual chain of command.

What set Dhuwan apart was its texture. ARY News, revisiting the cast in 2017, noted how the serial was praised for its realistic action at a time when Pakistani television rarely attempted action at all, and reported a widely held belief that it drew young viewers towards careers in the police. Nayyar Ejaz, Nazli Nasr, Asal Din Khan and Zubair Khan Achakzai filled out a team that felt like a real squad rather than a hero and his sidekicks. It showed that the state channel could do a thriller as well as a family drama. We could not find an official PTV upload, so no watch link is given here.

5. Alpha Bravo Charlie — 1998, PTV (with ISPR)

  • First broadcast: 1998
  • Channel: PTV (with ISPR)
  • Writer: Not confirmed by our sources
  • Director: Shoaib Mansoor
  • Producer: ISPR with PTV
  • Episodes: Not confirmed by our sources
  • OST: No credited soundtrack confirmed by our sources
  • Where to watch: No official upload found

Three young officers, one Mercedes and a helicopter stunt: Alpha Bravo Charlie is the late-1990s PTV serial that made army life look like the best adventure going. Shoaib Mansoor made it with ISPR, and the friendship between Faraz Inam's easy-going Faraz, Abdullah Mehmood's daredevil Kashif Kirmani and Qasim Khan's steady Gulsher Khan drives every episode. Shahnaaz Khawaja's Shahnaaz, a teacher of special children and Kashif's cousin, is the voice of sense among them.

The production went where Pakistani television had not gone before. The News has described shoots in the northern areas, including Siachen, and in Bosnia, where Gulsher is posted. The same report noted that Faraz Inam played a character called Faraz in Sunehray Din, Alpha Bravo Charlie and, decades later, Ehd-e-Wafa, which makes him the only actor to carry one role across three serials. Spoiler: The News recalls that audiences could not forgive Shoaib Mansoor for Gulsher's death late in the story. We found the serial only on channels we could not confirm as official, so there is no watch link here.

6. Raahein (Rahain) — 1998–99, PTV (Lahore)

  • First broadcast: 1998–99
  • Channel: PTV (Lahore)
  • Writer: Mansha Yaad
  • Director: Tariq Jameel
  • Episodes: 16 parts on PTV Digital (15 numbered episodes and a last episode)
  • OST: No credited soundtrack confirmed by our sources
  • Where to watch: PTV Digital (YouTube) (Episode 1: about 1.5 million views as of 1 October 2026)

The drama listed by many viewers as "Raahein" is PTV Lahore's Rahain, written by the Punjabi and Urdu fiction writer Mansha Yaad and directed by Tariq Jameel. The Express Tribune's obituary of Mansha Yaad in 2011 singled it out as his memorable PTV serial, based on his award-winning Punjabi novel Tanwan Tanwan Tara. PTV Digital dates it to 1998; some other listings give 1999.

Rahain is the least glamorous drama on this list and possibly the most honest. Its Punjab is a place of feudal pressure, injustice and illiteracy. The stories braid together: a student caught between his village and the city, a kabaddi player who loses his leg, and a young woman whose life falls apart after she leaves home. Tauqeer Nasir's Jura Bhatti and Saleem Sheikh's Khalid are at the centre, but the serial belongs to its crowd of village characters. It also seems to have found a new audience online: the first episode on PTV Digital had about 1.5 million views as of 1 October 2026, the highest of the PTV-era episodes we checked.

11. Waris — 1979, PTV (Lahore)

  • First broadcast: 1979
  • Channel: PTV (Lahore)
  • Writer: Amjad Islam Amjad
  • Director: Nusrat Thakur and Ghazanfar Ali
  • Episodes: 21 parts on PTV Digital's upload
  • OST: No credited soundtrack confirmed by our sources
  • Where to watch: PTV Digital (YouTube) (Episode 1: about 490,000 views as of 1 October 2026)

Dawn tells a story about Waris that sums up its hold on the country in late 1979: airline pilots reportedly refused to go into the cockpit until they had seen the episode. Amjad Islam Amjad's serial is about Chaudhry Hashmat Khan, the feudal lord of Sikandarpur, whose ancestral land lies in the path of a government dam. He fights the state, and then, more bitterly, his own son and grandsons, as the question of who will inherit his power tears the family apart.

Mehboob Alam played Hashmat so completely that Dawn described him as branded for life by the role. Around him sits one of the strongest ensembles PTV ever assembled, including Abid Ali, Uzma Gilani, Munawwar Saeed, Firdous Jamal, Samina Ahmed and Shujaat Hashmi. It was a hit at home and abroad at a time when it had to compete with Amritsar TV signals and the new VCR. Waris rewards patience. Its politics are slow and its silences long, but the central clash between an old order and a changing country has hardly dated.

12. Sunehray Din — 1991, PTV (with ISPR)

  • First broadcast: 1991
  • Channel: PTV (with ISPR)
  • Writer: Shoaib Mansoor
  • Director: Shoaib Mansoor (co-directed by Mohsin Ali)
  • Producer: ISPR in collaboration with PTV
  • Episodes: Not confirmed by our sources
  • OST: No credited soundtrack confirmed by our sources
  • Where to watch: ISPR Official (YouTube) (Part 1: about 276,000 views as of 1 October 2026)

Before Alpha Bravo Charlie there was Sunehray Din, the 1991 serial that turned army cadet life into prime-time viewing. Shoaib Mansoor wrote it and co-directed it with Mohsin Ali, ISPR produced it with PTV, and it became a hit straight after its first episode. It was shot on real military locations, which gave it a look that studio dramas of the time could not match.

The cast was almost entirely new. The News points out that it introduced Saleem Sheikh, Faraz Inam, Qasim Khan, Kiran and Aliya Kazmi to viewers, with Faraz Inam first seen as Faraz on a railway platform, a scene that became the start of a three-serial run for the character. The show more or less invented a genre. Later military dramas, from Alpha Bravo Charlie to Ehd-e-Wafa, borrowed its mix of barracks humour, rivalry and duty. The production values are modest by today's standards, but the energy of a young cast finding its feet comes through. ISPR Official has the serial on YouTube in parts.

13. Andhera Ujala — mid-1980s, PTV (Lahore)

  • First broadcast: mid-1980s
  • Channel: PTV (Lahore)
  • Writer: Younis Javed
  • Director: Rashid Dar
  • Episodes: Episodic cases; PTV Digital has uploaded more than 20 episodes
  • OST: No credited soundtrack confirmed by our sources
  • Where to watch: PTV Digital (YouTube) (Episode 1: about 351,000 views as of 1 October 2026)

"Jaffer Hussain, yeh sab kya ho raha hai?" If that line rings a bell, Andhera Ujala probably played in your house. The PTV Lahore police series, written by Younis Javed and directed by Rashid Dar, followed a single station through a new case almost every week, from petty theft to organised crime. Qavi Khan's DSP Tahir Ali Khan and Rahat Kazmi's ASP Asad-ur-Rehman led the team, but it was the station's two middle rankers who stole the show.

Jamil Fakhri's Inspector Jaffer Hussain was tough and dogged; in one episode, Dawn recalled, he shoots his own son in the leg. Irfan Khoosat's bumbling Havaldar Karamdad supplied the comedy. Dawn has reported that real police officers saluted Qavi Khan and still treated the cast with respect years later. The series is a good reminder of how PTV mixed public-service messages with entertainment, and since each episode stands mostly on its own it is easy to dip into on PTV Digital.

2000s–2010s Hits: Must-Watch Pakistani Dramas from the Private Channel Era

Private channels such as HUM, Geo and ARY changed the pace and look of Pakistani drama. These are the serials from that boom that people still recommend first.

7. Pyarey Afzal — 2013, ARY Digital

  • First broadcast: 2013
  • Channel: ARY Digital
  • Writer: Khalil-ur-Rehman Qamar
  • Director: Nadeem Baig
  • Episodes: 37 (ARY Plus)
  • OST: Title track "Jane Woh Kaise Log The" (singer not confirmed by an official source)
  • Where to watch: ARY Plus (official app) (Episode 1 on ARY Digital YouTube: about 6.5 million views as of 1 October 2026)

Afzal is a maulvi's son who wants to become a "bara admi", and he falls for Farah, a girl from a far richer family. Khalil-ur-Rehman Qamar's script turns that simple setup into a study of pride and class, and gives Afzal some of the most quoted lines of the 2010s, among them "Meri auqaat nahi hai ke mein Farah se muhabbat kar sakun, lekin us se muhabbat karne ka haq hai mujhe."

Hamza Ali Abbasi shot to stardom as Afzal, as Images Dawn put it when it ranked the serial among Pakistan's ten most iconic dramas. Ayeza Khan played Farah, and the pair's chemistry was strong enough that, a decade later, fans treated Jaan-e-Jahan as a second chance for them. At the 2015 Lux Style Awards the drama took best TV play, best actor, best actress, best director (Nadeem Baig) and best writer, a clean sweep of the main television categories. Spoiler: the ending is tragic, and many viewers have still not made peace with it. All 37 episodes are on ARY Plus.

9. Humsafar — 2011, HUM TV

  • First broadcast: 2011
  • Channel: HUM TV
  • Writer: Farhat Ishtiaq (novel and screenplay)
  • Director: Sarmad Sultan Khoosat
  • Producer: Momina Duraid
  • Episodes: 23
  • OST: "Woh Humsafar Tha", sung by Qurat-ul-Ain Balouch; music by Waqar Ali; lyrics by Naseer Turabi
  • Where to watch: HUM TV (YouTube) (Episode 1: about 4.3 million views; OST: about 12.7 million as of 1 October 2026)

If one serial explains why the 2010s are remembered as a second golden age, it is Humsafar. Farhat Ishtiaq adapted her own novel about Khirad and her wealthy cousin Ashar, who are married in haste. A marriage that begins as a duty slowly becomes a real romance, and is then pulled apart by family expectations, misunderstanding and separation. Sarmad Sultan Khoosat's direction keeps the camera close and the music low, so the big confrontations, when they come, land hard.

Images Dawn credits the serial with launching Fawad Khan and Mahira Khan to super-stardom, and it is hard to argue. Their pairing became the template for a decade of romantic leads. Naseer Turabi's lyrics for "Woh Humsafar Tha", sung by Qurat-ul-Ain Balouch, turned the OST into a hit of its own; HUM Music's upload had about 12.7 million views as of 1 October 2026. For anyone asking which is the best romantic Pakistani drama, this is the usual first answer. Read more on our Humsafar drama page.

14. Zindagi Gulzar Hai — 2012, HUM TV

  • First broadcast: 2012
  • Channel: HUM TV
  • Writer: Umera Ahmed
  • Director: Sultana Siddiqui
  • Producer: Momina Duraid
  • Episodes: 26
  • OST: HUM Music credits the OST to Hadiqa Kiani
  • Where to watch: HUM TV (YouTube) (Episode 1: about 3 million views as of 1 October 2026)

Kashaf Murtaza is not an easy heroine to love, and that is the point. Raised by a single mother and worn down by class and gender bias, she is sharp, defensive and suspicious of anyone with money, which puts her on a collision course with Zaroon Junaid, a charming, privileged college classmate. Umera Ahmed's story follows them into marriage, and becomes as much about class and pride as about romance.

Sanam Saeed played Kashaf without softening her, and Fawad Khan's Zaroon is funny, spoiled and quietly decent. Sultana Siddiqui, HUM's founder, directed. Images Dawn notes that the drama became very popular in India when Zee Zindagi broadcast it, It ran for 26 episodes, and HUM's upload of the first had about 3 million views as of 1 October 2026. If you liked Humsafar and want something with more bite and less melodrama, this is the obvious next choice. Our Zindagi Gulzar Hai page has more on the cast.

15. Dastaan — 2010, HUM TV

  • First broadcast: 2010
  • Channel: HUM TV
  • Writer: Samira Fazal (based on Razia Butt's novel Bano)
  • Director: Haissam Hussain
  • Producer: MD Productions
  • Episodes: 23
  • OST: Sung and composed by Sohail Haider
  • Where to watch: HUM TV (YouTube) (Episode 1: about 5.3 million views as of 1 October 2026)

Images Dawn put Dastaan at number one on its list of Pakistan's most iconic television dramas, and the choice is easy to understand. Adapted by Samira Fazal from Razia Butt's novel Bano and directed by Haissam Hussain, it follows Hassan and Bano, two young people in love on the eve of Partition, and what 1947 does to them. The first half is almost idyllic; the second is some of the most harrowing television HUM has aired.

Sanam Baloch gives Bano a quiet dignity that holds even in the serial's cruellest stretches, and Fawad Khan, a year before Humsafar, shows the restraint that later made him a star. Saba Qamar and Ahsan Khan lead a strong supporting cast. Sohail Haider sang and composed the soundtrack. It remains the best entry point for viewers who want to understand Partition through a family story rather than a history lesson. Expect to cry, and be warned that the violence is not softened.

Best Romantic Pakistani Dramas from This List

If you are here for love stories, start with Humsafar and Dhoop Kinare, then move to Zindagi Gulzar Hai, Sadqay Tumhare and Yakeen Ka Safar. Newer viewers tend to fall for Tere Bin, Ishq Murshid, Kabhi Main Kabhi Tum and Jaan-e-Jahan. Watch Pyarey Afzal before Jaan-e-Jahan; the two serials share a lead pair, and the second reads differently once you know the first.

For something lighter, Suno Chanda and Ankahi are comedies first. For social drama, Udaari, Raahein (Rahain) and Meri Zaat Zarra-e-Benishan are the strongest picks, and Sunehray Din, Alpha Bravo Charlie and Ehd-e-Wafa make a natural military trilogy.

Pakistani Drama Suggestions: FAQ

What is the best Pakistani drama of all time?

There is no single answer. Our first picks are Dhoop Kinare and Tanhaiyan from the PTV era and Humsafar from 2011. Images Dawn ranked Dastaan first in its list of iconic Pakistani dramas. For record-breaking reach, Khuda Aur Mohabbat Season 3 was reported by Geo in 2021 as the first Pakistani drama to cross one billion YouTube views.

What are the top 25 Pakistani dramas to watch?

Our list of 27 starts with Dhoop Kinare, Tanhaiyan, Ankahi, Dhuwan, Alpha Bravo Charlie, Raahein (Rahain), Pyarey Afzal, Parizaad, Humsafar and Ishq Murshid, followed by Waris, Sunehray Din, Andhera Ujala, Zindagi Gulzar Hai, Dastaan, Sadqay Tumhare, Udaari, Mere Paas Tum Ho, Suno Chanda, Ehd-e-Wafa, Tere Bin, Kabhi Main Kabhi Tum, Jaan-e-Jahan, Meri Zaat Zarra-e-Benishan, Diyar-e-Dil, Khuda Aur Mohabbat and Yakeen Ka Safar.

Which are the best romantic Pakistani dramas?

For romance, start with Humsafar, Dhoop Kinare, Zindagi Gulzar Hai, Sadqay Tumhare and Yakeen Ka Safar. For newer favourites, try Tere Bin, Ishq Murshid, Kabhi Main Kabhi Tum and Jaan-e-Jahan. Pyarey Afzal is one of the most loved, but be ready for a tragic ending.

What are the best old PTV dramas?

The classic PTV dramas most often recommended are Waris (1979), Ankahi (1982), Tanhaiyan (1986), Dhoop Kinare (1987), Sunehray Din (1991), Dhuwan (1994), Alpha Bravo Charlie (1998), Rahain (late 1990s) and the police series Andhera Ujala.

Where can I watch old PTV dramas online?

PTV's official YouTube channel, PTV Digital, has uploaded Dhoop Kinare, Tanhaiyan (Episode 1), Ankahi, Waris, Rahain and Andhera Ujala. ISPR Official hosts Sunehray Din. We could not find official uploads of Dhuwan or Alpha Bravo Charlie.

Where can I watch HUM, Geo and ARY dramas?

HUM TV, Har Pal Geo and ARY Digital upload full episodes of their dramas to their official YouTube channels, and ARY also has the ARY Plus app, where Pyarey Afzal is available in full. Each entry above links only to an official upload.

Who is in the Dhoop Kinare cast?

Dhoop Kinare stars Marina Khan as Dr Zoya and Rahat Kazmi as Dr Ahmer Ansari, with Sajid Hasan as Dr Irfan. It was written by Haseena Moin and directed by Sahira Kazmi.

Is the Humsafar drama based on a novel?

Yes. Humsafar is based on Farhat Ishtiaq's novel, and she also wrote the screenplay. It was directed by Sarmad Sultan Khoosat and produced by Momina Duraid for HUM TV in 2011.
