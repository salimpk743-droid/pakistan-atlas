Hyderabad District: Quick Answers

Hyderabad District is one of the 30 districts of Sindh, with its headquarters at Hyderabad. It has 4 talukas: Hyderabad City, Hyderabad, Latifabad and Qasimabad. The 2023 census counted 2,432,540 people on 993 km², and 67.21% of residents aged 10 and over were literate.

Hyderabad city: history

Hyderabad lies on the northernmost hill of the Ganjo Takkar ridge, just east of the Indus. It was founded in 1768 on the site of the ancient town of Nirun Kot by Ghulam Shah Kalhoro and named after Ali (Haidar). It remained the capital of Sindh under the Talpurs until 1843, when it surrendered to the British after the battles of Miani and Dabo and the capital moved to Karachi. It became a municipality in 1853. Its landmarks include the tombs of the Kalhora and Talpur rulers and a fort built in 1782, and the city is known for its badgirs, wind-catchers on rooftops (Britannica).

Talukas of Hyderabad District

The 2023 census counted 4 talukas in Hyderabad District: Hyderabad City, Hyderabad, Latifabad and Qasimabad. Latifabad is the most populous, with 800,983 people, and Hyderabad is the largest by area (711 km²). Literacy ranges from 42.57% in Hyderabad to 74.04% in Latifabad.

Population and growth

Hyderabad District had 2,432,540 people in the 2023 census, up from 2,199,928 in 2017 (average annual growth 1.69%). It covers 993 km². Of the 30 Sindh districts in the census, it ranks 6th by population and 24th by area. 83.14% of residents were counted in urban areas. There were 112.79 males for every 100 females.

Languages

Urdu was the most common mother tongue (45.92%), followed by Sindhi (43.12%), Punjabi (3.12%) and Pashto (2.83%).

Literacy and schooling

67.21% of people aged 10 and over were literate in 2023, 6th of the 30 districts in Sindh. Male literacy was 70.86% and female literacy 63.08%; the urban rate was 72.91% and the rural rate 37.1%. The census counted 211,452 children aged 5 to 16 out of school (100,728 girls and 110,710 boys).
