The character of the city

A Capital Inside a Natural Fortress

Quetta sits in a high valley surrounded by mountains. The setting is one of the defining features of the city: instead of spreading across an open plain, the urban landscape occupies a basin enclosed by dramatic ridges and peaks.

The Balochistan High Court describes Quetta as being surrounded by four major mountains — Chiltan, Takatu, Murdar and Zarghoon. The city stands at roughly 1,680 metres above sea level, giving it a cooler climate than many other major Pakistani cities.

Quetta's geography is not simply scenery. The mountains helped make the city a natural defensive position, a strategic crossroads and eventually the political heart of Balochistan.
Geography

The Valley Between Four Mountains

Quetta's physical geography explains much of its history. The city and surrounding district occupy a collection of valleys enclosed by mountain ranges. These natural barriers helped shape settlement, transport, agriculture and defence.

Chiltan

The Chiltan range forms one of the most recognizable mountain backdrops around Quetta and is closely associated with the city's identity.

Takatu

Takatu rises north of the urban area and contributes to the dramatic mountain setting of the Quetta basin.

Murdar

Murdar is another of the mountains forming the natural enclosure around Quetta.

Zarghoon

Zarghoon is part of the mountain landscape east of Quetta and helps define the city's wider physical environment.
