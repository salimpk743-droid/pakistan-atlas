Quetta city

The name Quetta comes from kwatkot, a Pashto word meaning "fort"; the city is still known locally as Shal or Shalkot. It stands at the north end of the Shal Valley, about 1,675 m above sea level, commanding the Bolan and Khojak passes. The British occupied Quetta in 1876, it became a municipality in 1896, and the Army Command and Staff College opened in 1907. An earthquake in May 1935 partly destroyed the city and killed about 20,000 people. The University of Balochistan was established there in 1970 (Britannica).

Administrative units of Quetta District

The 2023 census counted 5 administrative units in Quetta District: City (sub-division), Kuchlak (sub-division), Saddar (sub-division), Sariab (sub-division) and Panjpai (sub-tehsil). City is the most populous, with 1,360,600 people, and Saddar is the largest by area (1,283 km²). Literacy ranges from 37.67% in Panjpai to 63.97% in City.

Population and growth

Quetta District had 2,595,492 people in the 2023 census, up from 2,269,473 in 2017 (average annual growth 2.27%). It covers 3,447 km². Of the 34 Balochistan districts in the census, it ranks 1st by population and 26th by area. 60.32% of residents were counted in urban areas. There were 103.48 males for every 100 females.

Languages

Pashto was the most common mother tongue (59.95%), followed by Brahui (15.51%), Balochi (7.38%) and Punjabi (2.95%).

Literacy and schooling

56.29% of people aged 10 and over were literate in 2023, 2nd of the 34 districts in Balochistan. Male literacy was 65.06% and female literacy 46.96%; the urban rate was 62.95% and the rural rate 47.1%. The census counted 276,511 children aged 5 to 16 out of school (153,005 girls and 123,482 boys).
