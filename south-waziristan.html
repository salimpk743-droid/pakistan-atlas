South Waziristan

Divided in October 2022 into South Waziristan Upper and South Waziristan Lower · 888,675 people in the 2023 census

South Waziristan was a single tribal district of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa until October 2022, when it was divided into two: South Waziristan Upper, headquarters Spinkai Raghazai, and South Waziristan Lower, headquarters Wana. The KP cabinet approved the division on 12 April 2022 (Dawn). The notification, under the Land Revenue Act 1967, followed in October and split the district along tribal lines: Upper is mainly Mehsud and Lower mainly Ahmadzai Wazir (Dawn).

Tehsils of Upper and Lower South Waziristan

Upper has two sub-divisions, Sarwakai and Ladha, and seven tehsils: Tiarza, Serwakai, Shawal, Ladha, Makin, Shaktui and Sararogha. Lower has one sub-division, Wana, and four tehsils: Wana, Shakai, Tolkhela (Toi Khulla) and Birmal (Dawn). In January 2025 the KP chief minister announced that Upper would be renamed Mahsud Waziristan (The News). We have not found a renaming notification.

2023 census: the undivided district

The 2023 census still counted South Waziristan as one district: 888,675 people on 6,620 km², up from 675,215 in 2017 (4.70% a year). PBS counted no urban population, and there were 107.08 males for every 100 females (PBS Table 1). Pashto was the mother tongue of 870,805 people (97.99%) (PBS Table 11). Literacy (age 10 and over) was 31.96%: 42.63% for men and 20.38% for women (PBS Table 12).

The six census tehsils now in Upper add up to 488,438 people, with literacy of 38.88%. The three now in Lower add up to 400,237, with literacy of 22.99%. Shawal and Shakai have no separate census rows.

Operation Rah-e-Nijat (2009)

In October 2009, after about four months of preparation, the army launched Rah-e-Nijat ("Path to Deliverance"), a ground offensive against Taliban militants in what was then South Waziristan Agency. The ground offensive began on 17 October. The military said 30,000 troops took part, though some reports put the total at 60,000 (CTC Sentinel).
