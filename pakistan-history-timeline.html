Pakistan History Timeline: 1947 to the Present

Key events since independence, with dates from the National Assembly and other cited sources.

Pakistan came into being on 14 August 1947, when power was transferred from British India. Its first constitution came into force on 23 March 1956, East Pakistan became Bangladesh in December 1971, and the present Constitution has been in force since 14 August 1973. This timeline lists 74 key events from 1947 to 2026. Each entry names its source.

Exact dates come from the National Assembly of Pakistan's parliamentary history where it gives them. Where only a month or year is shown, the source gives no exact day. For every head of government and head of state, see the list of prime ministers and the list of presidents.

1947–1958: Independence and the first constitution

  • 14 August 1947: Transfer of power: Pakistan comes into being. Lord Mountbatten addresses the Constituent Assembly in Karachi, and Quaid-i-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah replies. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 15 August 1947: Jinnah is sworn in as the first Governor-General; Liaquat Ali Khan becomes the first prime minister. (National Assembly of Pakistan; National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 30 September 1947: Pakistan is admitted to the United Nations. (United Nations)
  • 11 September 1948: Jinnah dies in office as Governor-General. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 12 March 1949: The Constituent Assembly adopts the Objectives Resolution, moved by Liaquat Ali Khan. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 16 October 1951: Prime Minister Liaquat Ali Khan is assassinated; Khawaja Nazimuddin takes over the next day. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 24 October 1954: Governor-General Ghulam Muhammad dissolves the first Constituent Assembly. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 23 March 1956: The first Constitution comes into force and Pakistan becomes an Islamic Republic, with Iskander Mirza as its first president. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 7 October 1958: President Iskander Mirza abrogates the Constitution and declares martial law, with General Ayub Khan as Chief Martial Law Administrator. (National Assembly of Pakistan)

1958–1971: Ayub Khan, Yahya Khan and the break-up of Pakistan

  • 27 October 1958: General Ayub Khan takes over as president. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 1 March 1962: A new Constitution with a presidential system is given to the country. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 1965: Second war with India over Kashmir. (BBC News)
  • 25 March 1969: Second martial law: General Yahya Khan takes over as president. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 7 December 1970: The first general elections on adult franchise are held. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • December 1971: East Pakistan secedes and becomes Bangladesh after a civil war in which India intervenes. (BBC News)
  • 20 December 1971: Zulfikar Ali Bhutto takes over as president and Chief Martial Law Administrator. (National Assembly of Pakistan)

1972–1977: The 1973 Constitution

  • 1972: The Simla peace agreement with India sets a new frontline in Kashmir. (BBC News)
  • 10 April 1973: The National Assembly unanimously passes the Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 14 August 1973: The 1973 Constitution comes into force; Bhutto becomes prime minister and Fazal Ilahi Chaudhry president. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 5 July 1977: After a crisis over the March 1977 elections, General Zia-ul-Haq imposes martial law. (National Assembly of Pakistan)

1977–1988: Zia-ul-Haq

  • 1979: Zulfikar Ali Bhutto is hanged. (BBC News)
  • 25 February 1985: Non-party general elections are held; Muhammad Khan Junejo becomes prime minister. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • August 1988: General Zia is killed in an air crash. (BBC News)
  • 2 December 1988: Benazir Bhutto takes oath as prime minister. (National Assembly of Pakistan)

1988–1999: Elected governments and nuclear tests

  • 6 November 1990: Nawaz Sharif becomes prime minister for the first time. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 19 October 1993: Benazir Bhutto returns as prime minister. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 17 February 1997: Nawaz Sharif begins his second term as prime minister. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 1998: Pakistan conducts nuclear tests after India's tests. (BBC News)
  • May 1999: Kargil conflict with India. (BBC News)
  • 12 October 1999: General Pervez Musharraf takes over the government and declares himself Chief Executive. (National Assembly of Pakistan)

1999–2008: Musharraf

  • 20 June 2001: Musharraf assumes the office of president. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 10 October 2002: General elections are held for the 12th National Assembly. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • October 2005: An earthquake kills tens of thousands of people in Azad Kashmir. (BBC News)
  • 27 December 2007: Benazir Bhutto is assassinated at an election rally in Rawalpindi. (National Assembly of Pakistan; BBC News)
  • 18 August 2008: Musharraf resigns as president; Asif Ali Zardari is elected president on 6 September 2008. (National Assembly of Pakistan)

2008–2018: The 18th Amendment and the first democratic transfer

  • 19 April 2010: The 18th Amendment becomes part of the Constitution after the president's assent. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • August 2010: The worst floods in 80 years kill at least 1,600 people and affect more than 20 million. (BBC News)
  • May 2011: Osama bin Laden is killed by US special forces in Abbottabad. (BBC News)
  • 11 May 2013: General elections are held, and power passes from one elected government to another, which the National Assembly calls a historic milestone. Nawaz Sharif takes oath on 5 June 2013. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • October 2014: Malala Yousafzai, shot by the Taliban in 2012, becomes the youngest person to win the Nobel Peace Prize. (BBC News; The Nobel Prize)
  • December 2014: Militants kill nearly 150 people, mostly children, at a school in Peshawar. (BBC News)
  • 28 July 2017: The Supreme Court disqualifies Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif; Shahid Khaqan Abbasi is elected on 1 August. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 31 May 2018: The 25th Amendment merges the Federally Administered Tribal Areas into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. (Dawn)

2018 to the present

  • 18 August 2018: Imran Khan takes oath as prime minister after the July 2018 elections. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 10 April 2022: Imran Khan becomes the first prime minister removed by a no-confidence vote; Shehbaz Sharif takes office on 11 April. (Dawn; National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 2022: Monsoon rains and floods from mid-June kill 1,739 people by early November, according to the National Disaster Management Authority. (Xinhua)
  • 5 August 2023: The Council of Common Interests approves the results of the 2023 digital census. (Dawn)
  • 14 August 2023: Anwaar-ul-Haq Kakar is sworn in as caretaker prime minister. (Dawn)
  • 8 February 2024: General elections are held. (Dawn)
  • 3 March 2024: Shehbaz Sharif begins his second term as prime minister. (National Assembly of Pakistan)
  • 10 March 2024: Asif Ali Zardari is sworn in as president for a second time. (Dawn)
  • 21 October 2024: The 26th Constitutional Amendment, mostly about the judiciary, becomes law. (Dawn)
  • 10 May 2025: Pakistan and India agree to a ceasefire after military exchanges. (Dawn)
  • 11 November 2025: A suicide bomber kills 12 people and injures 36 outside the district and sessions courts in Islamabad’s G-11 sector. (Dawn; BBC News)
  • 13 November 2025: President Zardari assents to the 27th Constitutional Amendment, which creates the Federal Constitutional Court. (Dawn)
  • 14 November 2025: Justice Aminuddin Khan is sworn in by President Zardari as the first Chief Justice of the Federal Constitutional Court. (Dawn; APP)
  • 17 November 2025: A no-confidence motion removes Azad Jammu and Kashmir Prime Minister Chaudhry Anwarul Haq; PPP’s Faisal Mumtaz Rathore becomes AJK prime minister and is sworn in the next day. (Dawn; Dawn)
  • 5 December 2025: The Ministry of Defence notifies Field Marshal Asim Munir as Pakistan’s first Chief of Defence Forces, concurrently Chief of Army Staff, for five years. (Dawn; Geo News)
  • 8 December 2025: The IMF Executive Board completes the second review of Pakistan’s $7 billion Extended Fund Facility and the first review of its climate facility, releasing about $1.2 billion. (IMF; Dawn)
  • 20 December 2025: A special court sentences former prime minister Imran Khan and Bushra Bibi to 17 years each in the Toshakhana-2 case. (Dawn; APP)
  • 21 December 2025: Pakistan win the ACC Men’s Under-19 Asia Cup for the first time outright, beating India by 191 runs in the Dubai final. (ESPNcricinfo)
  • 28 February 2026: Pakistan beat Sri Lanka by five runs in Pallekele but are knocked out of the T20 World Cup in the Super 8s on net run rate. (BBC Sport; Dawn)
  • 6 March 2026: The men’s hockey team beat Japan 4–3 at the qualifier in Ismailia, Egypt, to reach the Hockey World Cup for the first time since 2018. (Dawn; APP)
  • 3 May 2026: Peshawar Zalmi win the eleventh Pakistan Super League, beating Hyderabad Kingsmen by five wickets in the final in Lahore. (Dawn)
  • 22 June 2026: After the 7 June Gilgit-Baltistan Assembly elections, in which PPP won the most seats, PPP’s Amjad Hussain is elected chief minister unopposed. (Dawn; Geo News)
  • 23 June 2026: The National Assembly passes the federal budget for 2026–27, an outlay of about Rs18.8 trillion, after an opposition walkout; the President assents on 26 June. (Dawn; Dawn)
  • 28 July 2026: Fatima Zahra becomes the first Pakistani woman boxer to secure a Commonwealth Games medal, reaching the 60 kg semi-finals in Glasgow; she takes bronze. (Dawn; Glasgow 2026)
  • 28 August 2026: After Azad Jammu and Kashmir elections held in phases from 27 July to 10 August, PML-N’s Syed Iftikhar Ali Gillani is elected and sworn in as AJK prime minister with 30 of 44 votes. (Dawn)
  • 28 August 2026: Pakistan finish 11th at the Hockey World Cup in Belgium and the Netherlands, beating South Africa in a shootout in Amstelveen. (Geo News; APP)
  • 3 September 2026: Pakistan raises $3 billion through a dual-tranche Eurobond, which the Finance Ministry calls the country’s largest single international bond deal. (The News; Radio Pakistan)
  • 24 September 2026: PML-N’s Dr Muhammad Najeeb Naqi is elected President of Azad Jammu and Kashmir with 32 votes. (Radio Pakistan; Dawn)
  • 26 September 2026: A suicide attack on a police and customs checkpoint in Darazinda, Dera Ismail Khan, kills 14 people. (Dawn; Dawn)
  • 28 September 2026: The NDMA’s monsoon report records 199 deaths and 552 injuries from rain and floods since 26 June 2026. (NDMA via ReliefWeb)
  • 29 September 2026: Shehbaz Sharif becomes the first Pakistani prime minister to open a trading session at the London Stock Exchange, marking the listing of the $3 billion Eurobond. (Geo News; APP)

FAQ

When did Pakistan gain independence?

On 14 August 1947, when power was transferred and Lord Mountbatten addressed the Constituent Assembly in Karachi. Jinnah was sworn in as Governor-General on 15 August 1947 (National Assembly of Pakistan).

When did Pakistan become a republic?

On 23 March 1956, when the first Constitution came into force and Pakistan became an Islamic Republic. That is why 23 March is also Republic Day (National Assembly of Pakistan).

When did Pakistan join the United Nations?

On 30 September 1947 (United Nations).

When did Pakistan become a nuclear power?

Pakistan carried out its nuclear tests in 1998, after India's tests that year (BBC News).

Who was the president of Pakistan in 1971?

General Yahya Khan, until 20 December 1971, when Zulfikar Ali Bhutto took over as president and Chief Martial Law Administrator (National Assembly of Pakistan).

How many constitutions has Pakistan had?

Three: 1956, 1962 and 1973. An interim constitution was also adopted on 17 April 1972. The 1973 Constitution is still in force (National Assembly of Pakistan).
