Rawalpindi

Capital of Pakistan 1959–1969 · headquarters Rawalpindi · 7 tehsils · 6,118,911 people (2023 census)

Rawalpindi District: Quick Answers

Rawalpindi District is one of the 41 districts of Punjab, in Rawalpindi Division, with its headquarters at Rawalpindi. The 2023 census lists 7 administrative units in it: Gujar Khan, Kahuta, Kallar Sayaddan, Kotli Sattian, Murree, Rawalpindi and Taxila. The 2023 census counted 6,118,911 people (pre-split boundaries) on 5,285 km², and 83.22% of residents aged 10 and over were literate.

Rawalpindi city: history

Rawalpindi was the capital of Pakistan from 1959 to 1969. It lies on the Potwar Plateau, 14 km southwest of Islamabad. The name means "village of Rawals", after a group of ascetics who lived at the site. Destroyed during the Mongol invasions of the 14th century, the town was restored by the Gakhar chief Jhanda Khan, who gave it its present name. It grew after the Sikh Milka Singh occupied it in 1765, was annexed by the British in 1849 and became a municipality in 1867. The Leh River separates the city from the cantonment, and Rawalpindi is the headquarters of the Pakistan Army (Britannica).

Tehsils of Rawalpindi District

The 2023 census counted 7 tehsils in Rawalpindi District: Gujar Khan, Kahuta, Kallar Sayaddan, Kotli Sattian, Murree, Rawalpindi and Taxila. Rawalpindi is the most populous, with 3,744,590 people, and it is also the largest by area (1,682 km²). Literacy ranges from 79.72% in Gujar Khan to 88.2% in Kotli Sattian.

Population and growth

Rawalpindi District had 6,118,911 people in the 2023 census, up from 5,402,380 in 2017 (average annual growth 2.1%). It covers 5,285 km². Of the 36 Punjab districts in the census, it ranks 3rd by population and 16th by area. 68.82% of residents were counted in urban areas. There were 103.39 males for every 100 females.

Towns and languages

The census lists 13 urban localities; the largest are Rawalpindi (2,284,014), Rawalpindi Cantonment (740,483), Wah Cantonment (400,733) and Chaklala Cantonment (333,115). Punjabi was the most common mother tongue (59.37%), followed by Urdu (16.98%), Pashto (10.41%) and Hindko (3.91%).

Literacy and schooling

83.22% of people aged 10 and over were literate in 2023, 1st of the 36 districts in Punjab. Male literacy was 87.9% and female literacy 78.36%; the urban rate was 84.41% and the rural rate 80.66%. The census counted 239,415 children aged 5 to 16 out of school (118,566 girls and 120,831 boys).

Rawalpindi District: FAQ

What is the population of Rawalpindi District?

The 2023 census counted 6,118,911 people in Rawalpindi District (pre-split boundaries), up from 5,402,380 in 2017 (annual growth 2.1%). 68.82% of the population lived in urban areas.

What is the area of Rawalpindi District?

Rawalpindi District covers 5,285 km² according to the 2023 census, a density of 1,157.79 people per km².

What are the main towns and cities in Rawalpindi District?

The 2023 census lists 13 urban localities in Rawalpindi District; the ten largest are Rawalpindi (2,284,014, metropolitan corporation), Rawalpindi Cantonment (740,483, cantonment), Wah Cantonment (400,733, cantonment), Chaklala Cantonment (333,115, cantonment), Gujar Khan (103,284, municipal committee), Taxila (75,444, municipal committee), Kahuta (75,349, municipal committee), Taxila Cantonment (61,456, cantonment), Kallar Syedan (60,941, municipal committee) and Daultala (27,237, municipal committee).

What is the literacy rate of Rawalpindi District?

83.22% of people aged 10 and over were literate in the 2023 census (male 87.9%, female 78.36%).

Which languages are spoken in Rawalpindi District?

The most common mother tongue in the 2023 census was Punjabi (59.37% of the 6,058,540 people in the mother-tongue table), followed by Urdu (16.98%), Pashto (10.41%), Hindko (3.91%).

Which province is Rawalpindi District in?

Rawalpindi District is in Punjab, in Rawalpindi Division. Its headquarters is Rawalpindi.

When was Rawalpindi the capital of Pakistan?

From 1959 to 1969. (Encyclopaedia Britannica)
