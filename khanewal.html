Khanewal District

Multan Division · junction on the Karachi–Peshawar line · phone 065

Khanewal is a railway fact that became a district. Before 1911 the headquarters was a village. A railway colony in 1904 and the Wazirabad–Khanewal and Khanewal–Lodhran lines turned it into a junction. Straight colonial streets still show that plan. On 1 July 1985 the file was cut out of Multan: Kabirwala and Mian Channu tehsils plus the newer units of Khanewal and Jahanian. The name is local — Daha settlers who used “Khan” in their names, hence Khan-e-wal — not a Mughal title invented for a brochure.

2023 census, one block. Population 3,364,077 on 4,349 km². Density about 774 per km². Households 526,196. Urban 716,786 (21 percent). Rural 2,647,291. Under-tens about 907,000. Literacy at ten and above: 60.97 percent (men 69.26, women 52.18). That female row is the education sentence. Official site: khanewal.punjab.gov.pk.

Four tehsils

Kabirwala — 1,804 km², 1,119,229 people, literacy 54.13 percent. Largest tehsil by land and by headcount. Weakest school row. Town about 92,000. Named for Baba Pir Kabir. Holds Tulamba, Sarai Sidhu and the Khalid Walid shrine. This is the old Multan north, not the junction.

Khanewal — 784 km², 987,445 people, literacy 63.60 percent. Headquarters. City municipal count about 281,890 in 2023. DHQ, the yards, the colleges that sit next to the line.

Mian Channu — 1,212 km², 872,581 people, literacy 64.39 percent. Named for the saint Baba Mian Channu. Town about 140,000. GT Road growth after 1972. Wyne country in the political memory.

Jahanian — 549 km², 384,822 people, literacy 65.65 percent. Smallest tehsil, highest literacy of the four. Town about 50,000. Canal-colony grid.

Four National Assembly seats, seven Punjab Assembly seats. Neighbours: Jhang and Toba Tek Singh north, Vehari south, Sahiwal east, Multan west.

People from this district

These are the names students already search. A roster is not a verdict on the living towns.

Har Gobind Khorana (1922–2011) — Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, 1968, shared for work on how the genetic code is read. Born in Raipur, a village now counted in Khanewal District, then in British Punjab. He studied at Punjab University, Lahore, left for England and then the United States, and spent his working life in Western laboratories. The district did not become a biochemistry campus because of him. The birth still belongs on this page. Students should know the name is from here and the prize was not won here.

Maulana Tariq Jamil — the preacher whose lectures travel on phone screens across Pakistan and the Gulf. Family and early years are tied to the Makhdoom Aali / Tulamba side of this district. Whatever a reader thinks of televangelism, he is the most widely heard voice this map has produced in the last thirty years.

Syed Fakhar Imam — Speaker of the National Assembly (1985–86), later federal minister, including National Food Security. A Syed political house with a long Kabirwala–Multan footprint. One of the few national parliamentary names that still points back to this district rather than only to Lahore.

Ghulam Haider Wyne (1940–1993) — Chief Minister of Punjab, 1990–93. From the Mian Channu belt. Killed in 1993. The CM years are the political high-water mark of the district in the federal-provincial story.

Syed Hussain Jahania Gardezi — Punjab minister in more than one cabinet, including management and professional development. The Gardezi / Jahania name is a Kabirwala-side political house still on the current list.

Tasawar Hayat — mathematician, Distinguished National Professor, known for work in fluid mechanics. A science name that does not need a shrine or a seat to stay on the page.

Local lists also print lawyers such as Malik Mohammad Aslam Metla, who pushed the 1985 district case. That is civic work, not celebrity. Christian villages and canal-colony settlements produced teachers and station staff whose names never reach Wikipedia. They still built the junction.

Cotton, cane, two rivers at a distance

Khanewal sells cotton first, then wheat, cane and fodder. It is Multan-division farm country with a station in the middle, not a mill city like Faisalabad. The Ravi system and old Sidhnai command water the north; the rest is canal-colony bar. Heat is the other crop: summers regularly pass 45°C. Winters can touch frost. Annual rain is thin — under 200 mm in a normal year — so the canal, not the cloud, decides the harvest.

Tulamba, in Kabirwala tehsil, is the oldest mark on the map: a mound and ruined walls that predate the railway by many centuries. The shrine of Khalid Walid near Kabirwala is the Ghaznavid-era tomb students are taken to photograph. Neither site made the junction. The junction made the district headquarters.

Speech

Punjabi about 2.37 million (70 percent). Saraiki about 653,000 (19 percent) — the Multan pull is audible here in a way Kasur never is. Urdu about 271,000 (8 percent), a Partition and railway-colony row. Pashto about 37,000. Christianity about 1.8 percent of the 2023 religion table, a canal-colony presence larger than most central-Punjab neighbours.

Schools and the DHQ

Government colleges in Khanewal, Kabirwala, Mian Channu and Jahanian. No public university of its own. Medicine, engineering and most honours seats still mean Multan (BZU, Nishter) or Sahiwal. DHQ Khanewal is the public hospital. THQs cover the other three tehsils. Specialist cases leave on the same line that built the town.

Towns on the sheet

Khanewal city, Mian Channu, Kabirwala, Jahanian, Abdul Hakim, Tulamba, Sarai Sidhu, Makhdoom Pur Pahoran.

Khanewal is a junction that learned to gin cotton and send a Nobel chemist, a chief minister, a Speaker and a preacher out of the same canal grid. The 2023 sheet is 3.36 million people and a girls’ literacy row still stuck near half. The trains still stop. The question is whether the colleges stop the next capable student from having to leave in order to be counted.
