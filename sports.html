Pakistan Sports: History, Major Titles and Latest Results (1947–2026)

Pakistan sports from 1947 to 2026 in one record: field hockey, widely regarded as the national sport of Pakistan, from the hockey fields of the 1950s to three Olympic golds and four World Cups; the cricket titles of 1992, 2009 and 2017; the squash dynasty of Hashim Khan, Jahangir Khan and Jansher Khan; and medals in athletics, boxing, wrestling, weightlifting, snooker and kabaddi. Recent highlights include Arshad Nadeem’s Olympic javelin gold in 2024 and, at the 2026 Asian Games, silver for the men’s cricket team and bronze for the men’s hockey team, the women’s cricket team and squash players Noor Zaman and Ashab Irfan. This page is a historical record of how Pakistan’s sporting identity was built — and how it changed.

Pakistan entered international sport as a new country in 1947 and made its Olympic debut at London 1948. The early decades established a pattern that still shapes the national story: field hockey became the country’s greatest Olympic team sport; squash produced an extraordinary line of world champions; cricket grew into the most followed game; and wrestling, boxing, athletics, weightlifting, snooker, kabaddi, sailing and wushu produced continental, Commonwealth and world-level medalists. The first Olympic medal came in hockey at Melbourne 1956. The first Olympic gold followed in Rome in 1960. Decades later, Arshad Nadeem became the first Pakistani athlete to win an individual Olympic gold medal, with a 92.97-metre javelin throw at Paris 2024.

Collective history

Pakistan Sports History Since 1947

Sport in the new state did not begin from nothing. Cricket, hockey, wrestling, polo and athletics already had deep roots in the cities, cantonments, clubs and akharas of the territories that became Pakistan. What changed after independence was the need to build national teams, federations and a public identity under a new flag. The Pakistan Olympic Association, later working with the Pakistan Sports Board, and sport-specific bodies such as the Pakistan Hockey Federation (1948) and the Board of Control for Cricket in Pakistan (1952; now the Pakistan Cricket Board) became the institutional frame.

The first international age was led by team sport. Hockey gave Pakistan an Olympic language almost immediately: fourth at London 1948 and Helsinki 1952, silver at Melbourne 1956, then gold at Rome 1960. That 1960 victory ended India’s long Olympic winning streak in men’s hockey and made the Green Shirts the country’s defining Olympic team. Through the 1960s, 1970s and 1980s hockey remained the sport by which Pakistan was most seriously measured abroad. Cricket, granted Test status in 1952, grew more slowly as a world force and then overtook hockey in popular attention after the 1992 World Cup.

Individual sport told a different story. Hashim Khan’s British Open titles in the 1950s opened a squash dynasty that, through Roshan Khan, Azam Khan, Qamar Zaman, Jahangir Khan and Jansher Khan, gave Pakistan a claim unique in world sport: decades of control at the top of a major individual game. Wrestling and boxing, drawing on pehlwani culture and amateur clubs, produced Pakistan’s first Olympic medals outside hockey: Muhammad Bashir in 1960 and Hussain Shah in 1988. Athletics had an early continental hero in Abdul Khaliq, the “Flying Bird of Asia,” long before Arshad Nadeem’s 2024 Olympic gold recast the country’s individual Olympic history.

The later twentieth century was a period of both glory and strain. Hockey won World Cups in 1971, 1978, 1982 and 1994, then declined as astroturf, professionalisation and administrative instability changed the world game. Cricket absorbed the national audience, especially after satellite television and the 1992 World Cup. Squash’s dominance faded as Egyptian and later other players took the world rankings. Women’s sport, long underfunded, became impossible to treat as a footnote after the Pakistan women’s cricket team’s Asian Games gold medals in 2010 and 2014 and the wider emergence of women in cricket, athletics, boxing, shooting and squash.

The twenty-first century has been a transition from historic team-sport dominance toward scattered but highly visible individual achievements: Sohail Abbas and later rebuild attempts in hockey; a succession of cricket generations from Inzamam-ul-Haq and Younis Khan through Misbah-ul-Haq, Sarfaraz Ahmed and Babar Azam; Muhammad Asif’s amateur world snooker titles; Muhammad Inam Butt in wrestling; Nooh Dastgir Butt in weightlifting; and Arshad Nadeem in the javelin. South Asian Games, Islamic Solidarity Games and Asian indoor and beach competitions have remained important stages even when Olympic medals were scarce. The honest record is not a straight line of triumph. It is a national archive of eras, declines, rebuilds and occasional historic days.

Olympic record

Pakistan at the Olympic Games: All 11 Medals

Pakistan’s Olympic history begins at London 1948. It should not be confused with Asian Games or Commonwealth medals. Across summer Games from 1948 through Paris 2024, Pakistan has won eleven Olympic medals: four gold, three silver and four bronze. Eight of those medals came in men’s field hockey. Only three were won by individual athletes.

The 1960 hockey gold is the founding Olympic legend: a 1–0 final win over India. Mexico City 1968 and Los Angeles 1984 completed a set of three hockey golds. After Barcelona 1992 there was a 32-year Olympic medal drought, broken at Paris 2024. Arshad Nadeem’s 92.97 m throw set an Olympic record, gave Pakistan its first individual Olympic gold, and was the country’s first Olympic gold in any sport since 1984. Paris 2024 was Pakistan’s nineteenth Summer Olympic appearance; the team was small, and the javelin gold stood almost alone against a long period of limited qualification.

Hockey also finished fourth at London 1948, Helsinki 1952 and Sydney 2000 — close, but not medals. Pakistan did not send a hockey team to every recent Games. That absence is part of the Olympic story, not a footnote to the golden era.

Continental record

Pakistan at the Asian Games

Pakistan missed the inaugural Asian Games in 1951 and has competed in every edition from Manila 1954 onward. Hockey has been the great medal engine: eight men’s gold medals (1958, 1962, 1970, 1974, 1978, 1982, 1990 and 2010), more than any other nation in the men’s tournament. Athletics produced an early golden generation around Abdul Khaliq, Muhammad Sharif Butt, Muhammad Nawaz and others at Manila 1954 and Tokyo 1958. Boxing and wrestling have been consistent medal sports. Sailing, often overlooked in popular memory, produced a remarkable Asian Games run from 1978, including golds for Byram D. Avari and Munir Sadiq.

Cricket entered the Asian Games in 2010. The Pakistan women’s team won gold at Guangzhou 2010 and again at Incheon 2014, both times defeating Bangladesh in the final. The men took bronze at Guangzhou 2010. Squash, snooker, kabaddi, wushu, rowing, weightlifting and cycling have added further medals in different editions. Performances have been uneven in the twenty-first century: strong nights in 2010 (including hockey gold and the cricket medals) contrasted with much thinner tallies in 2018.

South Asian Games and Islamic Solidarity Games have often been the next rung down: more medals, more sports, and an important stage for women athletes such as sprinter Naseem Hameed, who won the women’s 100 metres at the 2010 South Asian Games. Those medals are regional, not Olympic, and are recorded here as such.

Commonwealth record

Pakistan at the Commonwealth Games

Pakistan first competed at the 1954 British Empire and Commonwealth Games in Vancouver and was a regular medal-winning nation in wrestling, athletics, boxing and weightlifting before withdrawing from the Commonwealth between the mid-1970s and 1989. Wrestling has been by far the most productive Commonwealth sport, with a long list of gold medals from the 1958, 1962, 1966 and 1970 Games through later champions such as Azhar Hussain and Muhammad Inam Butt.

Athletics golds include Muhammad Iqbal in the hammer (1954), Ghulam Raziq in the 120 yards hurdles (1962), and Arshad Nadeem in the javelin at Birmingham 2022 — Pakistan’s first athletics gold at the Games since 1962. Weightlifting’s modern highlight is Nooh Dastgir Butt’s +109 kg gold at Birmingham 2022. Hockey won bronze at Manchester 2002 and silver at Melbourne 2006. Boxing has a thinner but real record, including Muhammad Waseem’s bronze at Delhi 2010. At Glasgow 2026, boxer Fatima Zahra won bronze in the women’s 60 kg division, a first Commonwealth boxing medal for a Pakistani woman.

Commonwealth medals are not Olympic medals. Several Pakistani champions who never stood on an Olympic podium still built substantial Commonwealth careers, especially in wrestling.

World titles, kept distinct

Pakistan World Champions: Cricket, Hockey, Squash and Snooker Titles

“World championship” is not a single event. Pakistan’s verified world-level titles sit in different governing bodies. Bilateral series wins, Asian titles and regional Games medals are not world championships.

Pakistan ICC Trophies: World Cup, T20 World Cup and Champions Trophy

Senior men’s ICC limited-overs titles: the 1992 Cricket World Cup, the 2009 ICC World Twenty20, and the 2017 ICC Champions Trophy. Pakistan were runners-up at the 1999 World Cup, the 2007 World Twenty20 and the 2022 T20 World Cup. These are ICC events. The Asia Cup (won in 2000 and 2012) is an Asian Cricket Council championship, not an ICC world title. The Under-19 World Cups of 2004 and 2006 are ICC youth world titles.

Pakistan Hockey World Cup Titles

Pakistan won the first Hockey World Cup in Barcelona in 1971, then again in 1978, 1982 and 1994 — four titles, still joint-most in the men’s event. Runners-up in 1975 and 1990. The Champions Trophy (1978, 1980, 1994) and Asia Cup (1982, 1985, 1989) are major events but are not the World Cup.

Pakistan Squash World Open Champions

Jahangir Khan won the World Open six times (1981–1985, 1988). Jansher Khan won it eight times (1987, 1989, 1990, 1992–1996), a men’s record. The British Open, which Hashim Khan won seven times, was the de facto world championship before the World Open era and remains a separate, older title.

Pakistan at the World Athletics Championships

Arshad Nadeem won silver in the men’s javelin at the 2023 World Athletics Championships in Budapest. Pakistan has not won a World Athletics gold.

Pakistan Snooker World Champions (IBSF)

Muhammad Asif has won the IBSF World Snooker Championship three times (2012, 2019, 2024). Ahsan Ramzan won the same amateur world title in 2021. These are amateur world titles organised by the International Billiards and Snooker Federation. They are not the professional World Snooker Championship staged at the Crucible Theatre. No Pakistani player has won that professional world title.

Wrestling and Other World Titles

Pakistan has won world-level medals in beach wrestling and other United World Wrestling events through athletes such as Muhammad Inam Butt, but it has not produced a cluster of Olympic-style senior world championship golds comparable to its Commonwealth wrestling record. Circle-style kabaddi has a “World Cup” won by Pakistan in 2020; that tournament is not the standard-style Kabaddi World Cup, which Pakistan has not won.

01 · Cricket

Pakistan Cricket Team: History, World Cup Wins and Records

Cricket is the sport most deeply embedded in Pakistan’s popular culture. The Board of Control for Cricket in Pakistan was formed in 1948 and the country was granted Test status in 1952. The first Test was played against India at Delhi in October 1952; India won by an innings and 70 runs. Pakistan’s first Test victory came in the second match of that series, at Lucknow, by an innings and 43 runs. Abdul Hafeez Kardar captained the early side. Fazal Mahmood became the great fast-bowling hero of the 1950s, notably when Pakistan beat England at The Oval in 1954. Hanif Mohammad became the defining batsman of the early Test era, including his 337 against West Indies at Bridgetown in 1958. Around them stood Imtiaz Ahmed, Wazir Mohammad, Saeed Ahmed and, soon after, the next generation: Mushtaq Mohammad, Majid Khan, Zaheer Abbas, Asif Iqbal and wicketkeeper Wasim Bari.

By the 1970s and 1980s Pakistan had a recognisable cricket identity: high-class batting, wrist-spin, and reverse-swinging fast bowling. Javed Miandad’s competitive batting, Imran Khan’s all-round cricket and captaincy, Abdul Qadir’s leg-spin, and the later emergence of Wasim Akram and Waqar Younis made Pakistan one of the most distinctive Test sides in the world. Salim Malik, Ramiz Raja and Inzamam-ul-Haq belong to the same broad era. This was still not, yet, a World Cup-winning country.

Pakistan 1992 Cricket World Cup Win Under Imran Khan

The defining popular moment came at the 1992 ICC Cricket World Cup in Australia and New Zealand. Captained by Imran Khan, Pakistan won only one of their first five matches (the one against England was washed out) and appeared close to elimination, then won three in a row to reach the semi-finals. They beat New Zealand in the semi-final and England in the final at Melbourne. Wasim Akram was the tournament’s leading wicket-taker, with 18 wickets. Inzamam-ul-Haq’s batting in the knockouts announced a future great. The squad’s remembered names also include Javed Miandad, Ramiz Raja, Aaqib Javed, Mushtaq Ahmed, Saleem Malik and Moin Khan. The victory was an ICC world title, not an Asian trophy, and it permanently changed cricket’s place in the national imagination.

1999 World Cup Final and the 2009 T20 World Cup Win

Pakistan remained a major limited-overs side through the 1990s. Saeed Anwar became one of the era’s celebrated opening batsmen. Saqlain Mushtaq, Mushtaq Ahmed, Moin Khan and Rashid Latif were central limited-overs figures. Pakistan reached the 1999 World Cup final and lost to Australia at Lord’s. That is a World Cup final, not a title.

Pakistan also won the inaugural Asian Test Championship in 1998–99, defeating Sri Lanka in the final at Dhaka after earlier beating India in the tournament. That is an Asian Test event, not an ICC World Test Championship. In the early 2000s Mohammad Yousuf, Shoaib Akhtar, Shahid Afridi, Abdul Razzaq, Younis Khan, Mohammad Hafeez and Shoaib Malik carried the side through a more turbulent period, including the first ICC World Twenty20 in 2007. Pakistan reached that inaugural T20 final and lost a close match to India in Johannesburg.

Two years later they won it. In the 2009 ICC World Twenty20 final at Lord’s, Pakistan beat Sri Lanka by eight wickets. Shahid Afridi scored an unbeaten 54. Younis Khan captained a versatile squad that included Umar Gul, Abdul Razzaq, Mohammad Hafeez, Kamran Akmal, Misbah-ul-Haq and Shoaib Malik. This is Pakistan’s second senior men’s ICC world title.

2017 Champions Trophy Win and Pakistan’s Modern Cricket Stars

The third senior men’s ICC limited-overs title arrived in the 2017 ICC Champions Trophy. Under Sarfaraz Ahmed, Pakistan recovered from a heavy group-stage defeat to India and won the final against India by 180 runs at The Oval. Fakhar Zaman scored a century in the final. Mohammad Amir took early wickets. Hasan Ali was the tournament’s leading wicket-taker, with 13 wickets. The side also included Babar Azam, Mohammad Hafeez, Shoaib Malik, Imad Wasim, Shadab Khan, Junaid Khan and Wahab Riaz. That victory completed a unique senior men’s set: World Cup, World Twenty20 and Champions Trophy.

Pakistan reached the men’s T20 World Cup final again in 2022 and lost to England in Melbourne. Babar Azam’s batting, Mohammad Rizwan’s keeping and batting, Shaheen Shah Afridi’s new-ball bowling, and the pace of Haris Rauf and Naseem Shah defined that campaign. The broader modern generation also includes Imam-ul-Haq, Fakhar Zaman, Shan Masood, Saud Shakeel, Azhar Ali, Yasir Shah, Mohammad Amir, Shadab Khan and Sarfaraz Ahmed. These are important international careers; they are not, by themselves, world championships.

Pakistan Asia Cup Wins and Test Record

Pakistan have won the senior men’s Asia Cup twice, in 2000 under Moin Khan and in 2012 under Misbah-ul-Haq. They have been runners-up in other editions, including 1986, 2014, 2022 and 2025. Asia Cup titles are Asian championships. Home and away Test series wins against England, Australia, India, West Indies, Sri Lanka, New Zealand, South Africa and others belong in the bilateral record, not in a list of world championships.

Pakistan Under-19 Cricket: World Cup and Asia Cup Titles

The Pakistan Under-19 side won consecutive ICC Under-19 World Cups in 2004 and 2006 — the first team to win the youth World Cup back to back. The 2004 title, in Bangladesh, was won against West Indies by 25 runs, with Khalid Latif as captain. The 2006 title, in Sri Lanka, was won against India; Sarfaraz Ahmed captained a side that was bowled out for 109 and then dismissed India for 71. Pakistan were also U19 World Cup runners-up in 1988, 2010 and 2014. The youth tournament has been a pathway for later senior players including Wahab Riaz, Fawad Alam, Imad Wasim, Anwar Ali, Babar Azam, Shaheen Shah Afridi and Shadab Khan.

In December 2025 Pakistan won the ACC Men’s U19 Asia Cup outright for the first time, defeating India by 191 runs in the Dubai final. Sameer Minhas scored 172. Pakistan had previously shared the 2012 U19 Asia Cup with India after a tied final. The 2025 title is an Asian youth championship, not an ICC World Cup.

Major Pakistan men’s cricket figures across generations: Abdul Hafeez Kardar, Fazal Mahmood, Hanif Mohammad, Imtiaz Ahmed, Saeed Ahmed, Mushtaq Mohammad, Majid Khan, Zaheer Abbas, Asif Iqbal, Wasim Bari, Javed Miandad, Imran Khan, Wasim Akram, Waqar Younis, Abdul Qadir, Aaqib Javed, Salim Malik, Ramiz Raja, Inzamam-ul-Haq, Saeed Anwar, Saqlain Mushtaq, Mushtaq Ahmed, Moin Khan, Rashid Latif, Shoaib Akhtar, Mohammad Yousuf, Shahid Afridi, Abdul Razzaq, Younis Khan, Mohammad Hafeez, Shoaib Malik, Misbah-ul-Haq, Umar Gul, Saeed Ajmal, Wahab Riaz, Sarfaraz Ahmed, Mohammad Amir, Azhar Ali, Yasir Shah, Babar Azam, Mohammad Rizwan, Fakhar Zaman, Imam-ul-Haq, Shadab Khan, Shaheen Shah Afridi, Naseem Shah, Haris Rauf, Shan Masood, Saud Shakeel, Abrar Ahmed, Mohammad Nawaz and other Pakistan internationals.

Women’s cricket

Pakistan Women’s Cricket Team: History and Stars

Organised Pakistan women’s international cricket is younger than the men’s game, but it is not a footnote. Shaiza Khan and Sharmeen Khan were the pioneers who helped establish a national side in the 1990s. Pakistan women played their first recorded One-Day Internationals in 1997. The early touring years were difficult, with heavy defeats and little domestic infrastructure, but they created the first generation of internationals.

Sana Mir became the defining figure of the next era. She captained Pakistan for eight years, led the side to Asian Games gold medals in 2010 and 2014, was the first Pakistan woman to take 100 ODI wickets, and in 2018 became the first Pakistani woman to be ranked the world’s No. 1 ODI bowler. In June 2025 she became the first Pakistani woman inducted into the ICC Cricket Hall of Fame. Bismah Maroof later captained with a batter’s authority; Nida Dar became the side’s leading all-round presence in T20 cricket; Javeria Khan, Sidra Amin, Aliya Riaz, Diana Baig, Nashra Sundhu, Muneeba Ali and Fatima Sana have been central to the 2010s and 2020s. Other important names from the longer record include Sadia Yousuf, Anam Amin, Nahida Khan, Asmavia Iqbal and Batool Fatima.

The Asian Games gold medals are the side’s clearest multi-sport titles. At Guangzhou 2010 Pakistan beat Bangladesh by ten wickets in the final. At Incheon 2014 they beat Bangladesh again in a rain-affected final. At Hangzhou 2022 the women reached the last four but did not medal. Pakistan women have not won an ICC Women’s World Cup or T20 World Cup. They have been regular ICC tournament participants and a consistent top-ten to top-twelve side rather than a world champion. That is the accurate measure: substantial, historic, still unfinished.

02 · Field hockey

Pakistan Hockey Team: Olympic Golds and World Cup Titles

Field hockey built Pakistan’s earliest international sporting identity. The Pakistan Hockey Federation was formed in 1948. The men’s team finished fourth at the 1948 and 1952 Olympics, won silver at Melbourne 1956, then gold at Rome 1960 by defeating India 1–0. Further Olympic golds came at Mexico City 1968 and Los Angeles 1984, with silvers in 1964 and 1972 and bronzes in 1976 and 1992. Air Marshal Nur Khan later helped drive the creation of the Hockey World Cup and the Champions Trophy, events in which Pakistan were not merely participants but founding powers.

Pakistan won the first Hockey World Cup, in Barcelona in 1971, and won it again in 1978, 1982 and 1994. Asian Games golds came in 1958, 1962, 1970, 1974, 1978, 1982, 1990 and 2010. The Asia Cup was won in 1982, 1985 and 1989. The Champions Trophy was won in 1978, 1980 and 1994. The Asian Champions Trophy was won in 2012, 2013 and 2018 (the 2018 title was shared with India after the final in Muscat was washed out). Commonwealth medals include bronze in 2002 and silver in 2006. These are separate competitions; stacking them together as a single “world record” would be misleading. Taken in their own categories, they still amount to one of Asia’s great team-sport dynasties.

The playing generations are as important as the titles. Abdul Hamid, Munir Dar, Manzoor Hussain Atif, Motiullah, Anwar Ahmed Khan, Islahuddin Siddiqui, Samiullah Khan — the “Flying Horse” — Hassan Sardar, Kaleemullah Khan, Akhtar Rasool, Shahbaz Ahmed, the “Maradona of hockey,” goalkeeper Mansoor Ahmed, and later penalty-corner specialist Sohail Abbas, at one time the world’s leading international goalscorer, mark different peaks. Waseem Ahmed became the most-capped player. The 1982 World Cup side and the 1984 Olympic champions sit at the heart of the golden era.

The decline is part of the same file. Astroturf changed a game Pakistan had mastered on grass. Other nations professionalised faster. Qualification failures, including missed World Cups in 2014 and 2023, and the absence of the team from several recent Olympics, ended the assumption that Pakistan would always be a medal favourite. Women’s hockey has existed as a national programme but has not reached the same international height as the men’s side. Rebuild efforts, including a return toward the World Cup pathway in the mid-2020s, belong to the present tense of the sport, not to the golden-era medal table.

03 · Squash

Pakistan Squash: Jahangir Khan, Jansher Khan and the Khan Dynasty

Few countries have dominated an individual sport as completely as Pakistan dominated squash in the second half of the twentieth century. Hashim Khan, a Pakistani professional from the Peshawar world of club markers, won the British Open seven times: 1951 through 1956, and again in 1958. In that era the British Open was the de facto world championship. He also won British Professional, US Open and Canadian Open titles. Roshan Khan won the British Open in 1957. Azam Khan won it four times, 1959 to 1962. Qamar Zaman won it in 1975 and was among the world’s leading professionals of that decade. Hiddy Jahan, a major figure of the 1970s and early 1980s, reached the British Open final, including the 1982 final against Jahangir Khan.

Jahangir Khan then produced one of the most extreme careers in any sport. He won the World Open six times (1981, 1982, 1983, 1984, 1985 and 1988) and the British Open ten consecutive times from 1982 to 1991. He went 555 competitive matches unbeaten between 1981 and 1986, a streak recorded by Guinness World Records. Jansher Khan succeeded and then surpassed him in World Open titles, winning a men’s record eight (1987, 1989, 1990 and 1992–1996) and six British Opens. Their rivalry meant that Pakistani players controlled the top of world squash for much of the 1980s and 1990s.

Later Pakistani professionals — including Zarak Jahan Khan, Aamir Atlas Khan, Farhan Mehboob and others — remained international players, but the era of automatic world No. 1s ended. The modern world tour has been led largely by Egyptian players. Pakistan’s squash history is therefore both a record of unmatched dominance and a warning about how quickly a national production line can thin.

Iconic squash names: Hashim Khan, Roshan Khan, Azam Khan, Mohibullah Khan, Qamar Zaman, Jahangir Khan, Jansher Khan, Hiddy Jahan, Zarak Jahan Khan, Aamir Atlas Khan and later international professionals who represented Pakistan.

04 · Athletics

Pakistan Athletics: From Abdul Khaliq to Arshad Nadeem

Pakistan’s athletics history has two widely separated peaks. The first was continental. Abdul Khaliq, remembered as the “Flying Bird of Asia,” won the Asian Games 100 metres in 1954 and 1958, with further relay and 200-metre medals. He competed at the Melbourne 1956 and Rome 1960 Olympics without reaching an Olympic final medal. Ghulam Raziq became a celebrated hurdler and won Commonwealth gold in the 120 yards hurdles in 1962. Muhammad Iqbal won Commonwealth hammer gold in 1954. Muhammad Nawaz, Jalal Khan, Mubarak Shah and others made the 1950s and 1960s the most productive Asian Games era for Pakistani track and field.

For decades after that, athletics remained a South Asian and occasional Asian medal sport without an Olympic podium. Naseem Hameed’s women’s 100 metres gold at the 2010 South Asian Games was an important national moment for women’s track and field, not an Olympic or World Athletics medal.

Arshad Nadeem Javelin Record: 92.97 m Olympic Gold at Paris 2024

The second peak is Arshad Nadeem. He finished fifth at the Tokyo 2020 Olympic final with 84.62 metres, won Commonwealth gold at Birmingham 2022, and took silver at the 2023 World Athletics Championships in Budapest. At Paris 2024 he threw 92.97 metres in the men’s javelin final, an Olympic record, to win gold. It was Pakistan’s first Olympic medal since 1992, first gold since 1984, and first individual Olympic gold. The previous Olympic record of 90.57 metres had stood since 2008. The 2024 throw was the event that moved athletics from a supporting chapter in this archive to a central one.

Pakistan has not won a World Athletics gold. Nadeem’s world championship medal is silver. That distinction matters: the Olympic title is the historic first; the world championship record remains incomplete.

05 · Wrestling

Pakistan Wrestling: Pehlwani Culture and Olympic Medals

Wrestling in Pakistan is two related traditions. Pehlwani, the akhara culture of dangals, oil, and kushti, is older than the state and remains a living popular sport, especially in Punjab. Modern Olympic freestyle wrestling is the international code in which Pakistan has actually won Games medals. The two should not be collapsed into one achievement list.

Muhammad Bashir won Pakistan’s first Olympic medal outside hockey: bronze in freestyle (73 kg) at Rome 1960. He was also a Commonwealth champion. The Commonwealth Games, not the Olympics, became wrestling’s great Pakistani stage. Gold medals arrived in clusters at Cardiff 1958, Perth 1962, Kingston 1966 and Edinburgh 1970. In the modern era Azhar Hussain won Commonwealth gold at Delhi 2010 (and a Greco-Roman silver at the same Games). Muhammad Inam Butt won Commonwealth gold in 2010 and 2018, and silver at Birmingham 2022. He has also won world-level titles in beach wrestling. Inam is a leading modern grappler; he is not an Olympic medalist.

Asian Games wrestling has produced gold, silver and bronze across decades, though not at the density of the Commonwealth record. South Asian Games wrestling has been even more productive. The honest Olympic file remains short: one bronze, in 1960.

06 · Boxing

Pakistan Boxing: Olympic and Commonwealth Medals

Hussain Shah won Pakistan’s only Olympic boxing medal: bronze in the middleweight division at Seoul 1988. That remains the ceiling of the Olympic file. Asian Games boxing has been far more productive, including Mehrullah Lassi’s gold at Busan 2002. Commonwealth boxing medals include Muhammad Waseem’s bronze at Delhi 2010. At Glasgow 2026, Fatima Zahra won bronze in the women’s 60 kg division, the first Commonwealth boxing medal for a Pakistani woman.

Professional boxing has produced internationally known Pakistani and Pakistan-origin fighters, but this archive is concerned with the national amateur and Olympic record. Women’s boxing is a developing programme rather than a historic medal factory. Pakistan has not won an Olympic boxing gold.

07 · Cue sports

Pakistan Snooker: Muhammad Asif and IBSF World Titles

Pakistan has a genuine world-level cue-sports record — at amateur, not professional Crucible, level. Muhammad Asif, a Pakistani professional and amateur champion, has won the IBSF World Snooker Championship three times: 2012 in Sofia, 2019 in Antalya, and 2024 in Doha. Ahsan Ramzan won the same amateur world title in 2021. Pakistani players have also won IBSF team and 6-red events. These titles are awarded by the International Billiards and Snooker Federation.

They should not be described as professional World Snooker Championship titles. No Pakistani player has won the ranking world championship at the Crucible. Muhammad Asif did play on the professional World Snooker Tour after turning professional, without becoming a ranking world champion. Asian Championships and Asian Games snooker medals form a second, continental layer of the same story. Cue sports are one of the few disciplines in which Pakistan has repeatedly produced world amateur champions in the twenty-first century.

08 · Kabaddi

Pakistan Kabaddi: Asian Games Medals and Circle-Style World Cup

Kabaddi is a traditional subcontinental game with a large following in Punjab on both sides of the border. International kabaddi is split between codes, and titles are easy to confuse. In the standard indoor game played at the Asian Games, Pakistan have won silver medals (including 1998 and 2006) and multiple bronzes, but have not won Asian Games gold. India have dominated that event. Pakistan have also not won the standard-style Kabaddi World Cup.

Circle-style kabaddi, closer to the traditional outdoor game, has a separate World Cup. India won the first several men’s editions. Pakistan won the 2020 circle-style World Cup in Lahore, beating India 43–41 in the final — the only non-Indian winner of that competition. Pakistan were runners-up in several earlier circle-style World Cups. The circle-style Asia Cup has also been won by Pakistan. Those are real titles. They are not the same tournament as the standard-style World Cup, and they are not Olympic medals. The India–Pakistan rivalry is the sport’s central international fact.

09 · Weightlifting

Pakistan Weightlifting: Nooh Dastgir Butt and Commonwealth Gold

Weightlifting has given Pakistan Commonwealth and occasional Asian medals, but not an Olympic medal. The outstanding modern result is Nooh Dastgir Butt’s gold in the men’s +109 kg at the 2022 Commonwealth Games in Birmingham. Earlier generations won Commonwealth medals in lighter weight classes. Pakistan has participated at Asian Games and world championships without becoming a sustained world power in the sport. The file is respectable at Commonwealth level and thin at Olympic level.

10 · Football

Pakistan Football Team: History and FIFA Record

Football is widely played and has a longer international history than its results suggest. The national team debuted in 1950. Pakistan have never qualified for the FIFA World Cup finals and have not qualified for the AFC Asian Cup finals in the modern championship era. Regional peaks include gold medals at the South Asian Games in 1989 and 1991, and an early title at the 1952 Asian Quadrangular Football Tournament. Those are regional results.

Unlike hockey and cricket, football has not produced a senior world or Asian championship for Pakistan. Club football, the Pakistan Premier League and youth programmes exist; they have not yet changed the senior international record. Honesty is the point of this entry.

11 · Badminton

Badminton in Pakistan

Pakistan has competed in badminton at South Asian Games, Islamic Solidarity Games, Asian and Commonwealth level, with occasional regional medals. It has not won an Olympic, World Championship or Asian Games gold in the sport. Badminton remains a participation and development sport in the national file rather than a historic medal sport.

12 · Table tennis

Table tennis in Pakistan

Table tennis has a domestic following and a national championship structure. Internationally, Pakistan has taken part in South Asian and other regional events without winning a major Olympic, world or Asian Games title. No such title is claimed here.

13 · Tennis

Tennis in Pakistan

Pakistan’s outstanding tennis career belongs to Aisam-ul-Haq Qureshi, a Lahore-born professional who specialised in doubles. He reached world No. 8 in doubles, won 18 ATP Tour doubles titles, and in 2010 became the first Pakistani to play a Grand Slam final, finishing runner-up in both men’s doubles (with Rohan Bopanna) and mixed doubles (with Květa Peschke) at the US Open. He did not win a Grand Slam title. Pakistan have not won a Davis Cup, and no Pakistani has won an Olympic tennis medal. Qureshi’s career is the country’s clearest tennis achievement.

14 · Swimming

Swimming in Pakistan

Pakistani swimmers have competed at Olympic, Asian, Commonwealth and South Asian level, often through universality places at the Olympics. Kiran Khan is among the better-known women swimmers of the 2000s. Pakistan has won South Asian medals but has not won an Olympic, World Aquatics or Asian Games swimming gold. Swimming’s national story is participation and regional competition, not world titles.

15 · Cycling

Cycling in Pakistan

Cycling appears in Pakistan’s Asian Games medal history, including track medals in earlier decades, and remains a national championship sport. Pakistan has not become a regular Olympic cycling medal contender. Road racing, track and mountain biking exist as domestic and occasional continental activities rather than as a world-title production line.

16 · Archery

Archery in Pakistan

Archery is an Olympic sport in which Pakistan has had limited international impact. Athletes have competed at regional events and sought Olympic qualification. Pakistan has not won an Olympic, world or Asian Games archery title of major note. The sport is recorded here as part of the national programme, not as a medal dynasty.

17 · Shooting

Shooting in Pakistan

Shooting has produced Commonwealth medals for Pakistan and a growing women’s presence. At Paris 2024, Kishmala Talat, Gulfam Joseph and Ghulam Mustafa Bashir competed after earning qualification marks — a contrast with universality places in other sports — but did not win Olympic medals. Pakistan has not won an Olympic shooting medal. Commonwealth and South Asian medals form the verified international layer.

18 · Judo

Judo in Pakistan

Judo is a regular Commonwealth and Asian programme sport for Pakistan. Shah Hussain Shah, son of Olympic boxing medalist Hussain Shah, won Commonwealth bronze at Birmingham 2022 in the men’s −90 kg. Pakistan has not won an Olympic judo medal. The sport’s international record is real at Commonwealth level and modest on the Olympic stage.

19 · Karate

Karate in Pakistan

Karate has brought Pakistan occasional Asian Games and Islamic Solidarity Games medals, including bronze at continental level. It has not produced an Olympic medal for Pakistan (karate was on the Tokyo 2020 programme). South Asian competition has been the more regular source of titles.

20 · Taekwondo

Taekwondo in Pakistan

Taekwondo is an Olympic sport with a national federation and regular South Asian and Islamic Solidarity Games participation. Pakistan has not won an Olympic taekwondo medal and has not been a leading Asian Games power in the sport. Regional medals exist; world titles do not.

21 · Wushu

Wushu in Pakistan

Wushu has been a useful Asian Games medal sport for Pakistan, with silver and bronze medals in recent editions. It is not an Olympic sport. The results are continental, and they are among the more consistent modern additions to Pakistan’s multi-sport tally, even if they rarely enter popular sporting memory beside cricket and hockey.

22 · Rowing

Rowing in Pakistan

Rowing has produced Asian Games bronze medals for Pakistan. The sport is based largely in a small high-performance group rather than a mass culture. Pakistan has not won an Olympic rowing medal. The Asian Games bronzes are the verified international highlight.

23 · Sailing

Sailing in Pakistan

Sailing is one of Pakistan’s most successful and least publicised Asian Games sports. Byram D. Avari and Munir Sadiq won Enterprise-class gold at Bangkok 1978. Avari retained the title at Delhi 1982 with Goshpi Avari. Munir Sadiq later won further golds with Muhammad Zakaullah in 1986 and 1990, and additional medals in 1994 and 1998, becoming the first sailor to win five Asian Games medals. Pakistan also won gold in the OK dinghy class in 1982. These are Asian Games titles, not Olympic medals. Munir Sadiq competed in the 470 at the 1984 Olympics without medalling. The sailing file is a reminder that Pakistan’s continental record is broader than hockey and cricket.

24 · Equestrian sports

Equestrian sports in Pakistan

Equestrian sport in Pakistan is centred on army, police and club traditions, including showjumping and tent pegging. Tent pegging has produced occasional multi-sport medals. Pakistan has not been an Olympic equestrian medal nation. The sport is historically real in garrison life and thin in the Olympic record.

25 · Polo

Polo in Pakistan

Polo is older in the northern mountains than most modern Olympic sports. Shandur and other high-altitude grounds in Gilgit-Baltistan and Chitral carry a living polo culture that is part of the national atlas as much as of the sports page. International arena polo and club polo exist, including army teams. Polo is not an Olympic medal sport for Pakistan, and this archive does not invent world championships around the festival game. Its importance is historical and cultural.

26 · Golf

Golf in Pakistan

Golf has clubs in the major cities and a national championship. Pakistani golfers have competed regionally. The country has not produced a major-championship winner or an Olympic golf medal. Golf is recorded as a participation sport with a thin international medal file.

27 · Basketball

Basketball in Pakistan

Basketball is played in schools, universities and the national championship. Pakistan has competed in regional events, including South Asian competition, without qualifying for an Olympic basketball tournament or winning a world or Asian championship. The sport’s significance is domestic.

28 · Volleyball

Volleyball in Pakistan

Volleyball has a national federation and a place in South Asian Games programmes, with occasional regional medals (including an Asian Games bronze in the historical table). Pakistan has not been a world or Olympic volleyball power. The sport is widely played and lightly decorated at the highest level.

29 · Handball

Handball in Pakistan

Handball is an Olympic sport in which Pakistan’s international footprint is small. National teams have appeared in regional competitions. There is no Olympic, world or Asian Games gold to record. Handball is part of the multi-sport programme, not of the medal dynasties.

30 · Rugby

Rugby in Pakistan

Rugby union and rugby sevens have a small organised presence, including a national sevens side at regional events. Pakistan have not qualified for a Rugby World Cup. The sport is growing from a low base and has no major world title in this archive.

31 · Chess

Chess in Pakistan

Chess has a national federation, rated players and Olympiad teams. Pakistan has not produced a World Chess Champion or a large cluster of grandmasters by global standards. The honest description is participation in FIDE events and gradual development, not a historic world-title story.

Women in Pakistani sport

Pakistani Women in Sport

Women’s sport is not a single late addition. It runs through cricket, athletics, boxing, shooting, squash, swimming and football, usually with less money, fewer domestic leagues and more social friction than the men’s programmes. The documented peaks include Sana Mir and the women’s cricket Asian Games golds of 2010 and 2014; Naseem Hameed’s South Asian Games sprint title in 2010; Kishmala Talat’s Olympic shooting qualification in 2024; and Fatima Zahra’s Commonwealth boxing bronze in 2026. Bismah Maroof, Nida Dar, Javeria Khan, Fatima Sana, Kiran Khan and others belong in the same national file as Imran Khan or Jahangir Khan, even when the medal cabinets are smaller.

The structural facts are as important as the names: later international debuts, thinner school sport, and a still-incomplete professional pathway. An archive that treated women’s sport only as a closing paragraph would misread the last two decades.

Hockey · 2026 update

Pakistan Hockey Team 2026: World Cup Return and Asian Games

The Pakistan men’s hockey team returned to the World Cup in 2026 for the first time since 2018. At the qualifier in Ismailia, Egypt, Ammad Butt’s side came from 3–1 down to beat Japan 4–3 in the semi-final on 6 March 2026, which guaranteed a place at the World Cup; England beat them in the qualifier final. In the nine-team 2025–26 FIH Pro League, which ended in June 2026, Pakistan lost all 16 of their matches and finished last with a goal difference of minus 57.

At the World Cup in Belgium and the Netherlands in August 2026 Pakistan finished 11th, their best World Cup finish since 2006, beating South Africa 4–3 in a penalty shootout in the 11th-place play-off in Amstelveen after a 4–4 draw. Germany won the tournament, beating Spain 1–0 in the final in Wavre on 30 August 2026. It was Germany’s fourth title (2002, 2006, 2023 and 2026), which equalled Pakistan’s record of four men’s World Cups (1971, 1978, 1982 and 1994); the two countries now share the record.

At the 2026 Asian Games Pakistan won four of their five pool matches, losing only to Malaysia (5–4), and beat Oman 7–2 on 28 September to reach the semi-finals, where they lost 4–3 to India on 1 October. Sufyan Khan was Pakistan’s leading scorer in the pool stage, mainly from penalty corners.

In the semi-final at the Gifu Prefectural Green Stadium in Kakamigahara, India led 3–0 through Harmanpreet Singh (5th and 13th minutes, both penalty corners) and Sukhjeet Singh (23rd). Pakistan drew level through Abu Mahmood (30th), Hannan Shahid (45th) and Waheed Rana (49th), but in the final minute Abhishek’s shot deflected off Sufyan Khan past goalkeeper Ali Raza for India’s winner. In the bronze-medal match at the same venue on 3 October, Pakistan came from 3–1 down to beat South Korea 5–3. Sufyan Khan, Abdul Rehman, Waheed Rana (two goals) and Hannan Shahid scored as Pakistan took bronze.

Questions

Pakistan Sports FAQ 2026

Who won PSL 2026?

Peshawar Zalmi won PSL 11 in 2026, beating Hyderabad Kingsmen by five wickets in the final at the Gaddafi Stadium, Lahore, on 3 May 2026. It was Zalmi’s second title, after 2017, and they were captained by Babar Azam.

Who is the captain of the Pakistan cricket team in 2026?

Pakistan have a different captain in each format: Babar Azam leads the Test side, Shaheen Shah Afridi the ODI side and Salman Ali Agha the T20I side. Sahibzada Farhan captains the team at the 2026 Asian Games.

Who is the head coach of the Pakistan cricket team?

Mike Hesson is the white-ball head coach and took interim charge of the Test side in September 2026, after red-ball coach Sarfaraz Ahmed was removed during the England series. Ashley Noffke is the bowling coach and Aaqib Javed the national selector.

How many cricket World Cups has Pakistan won?

Pakistan have won three senior men’s ICC titles: the 1992 Cricket World Cup, the 2009 World Twenty20 (T20 World Cup) and the 2017 Champions Trophy. Pakistan also won the ICC Under-19 World Cup in 2004 and 2006.

How did Pakistan do at the 2026 T20 World Cup?

Pakistan reached the Super 8s but were knocked out on net run rate. Sahibzada Farhan became the first batter to score two hundreds in one men’s T20 World Cup. India won the tournament.

What is Arshad Nadeem’s javelin record?

Arshad Nadeem threw 92.97 metres to win gold at the Paris 2024 Olympics. It is the Olympic record and his personal best, and it made him Pakistan’s first individual Olympic champion.

Did Arshad Nadeem win a medal at the 2026 Asian Games?

No. He finished fourth with 80.29 metres in Nagoya on 28 September 2026; Sri Lanka’s Rumesh Pathirage won gold. At the 2026 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow he finished ninth.

How many Hockey World Cups has Pakistan won?

Four: 1971, 1978, 1982 and 1994. Germany equalled that record with a fourth title in 2026, so Pakistan and Germany jointly hold the record for most men’s Hockey World Cup titles.

Did the Pakistan hockey team play in the 2026 World Cup?

Yes. Pakistan qualified by beating Japan 4–3 in Egypt in March 2026, their first World Cup since 2018, and finished 11th after beating South Africa in a penalty shootout.

What are Babar Azam’s biggest records?

Babar Azam was the fastest batter to 5,000 ODI runs (97 innings), became the leading run-scorer in men’s T20 Internationals on 31 October 2025, and in April 2026 became the first batter to reach 4,000 PSL runs.

How many Olympic medals has Pakistan won?

Pakistan have won 11 Olympic medals: four gold, three silver and four bronze. Eight came in men’s hockey; the others are Muhammad Bashir’s wrestling bronze (1960), Hussain Shah’s boxing bronze (1988) and Arshad Nadeem’s javelin gold (2024).

How many British Open titles did Jahangir Khan win?

Jahangir Khan won the British Open ten times in a row, from 1982 to 1991, and the World Open six times. Jansher Khan holds the men’s record of eight World Open titles.

Federations and the state

Pakistan Sports Institutions: PSB, POA, PCB and PHF

The Pakistan Sports Board is the federal sports body. The Pakistan Olympic Association (NOC Pakistan) selects Olympic and many multi-sport teams. The Pakistan Cricket Board governs cricket. The Pakistan Hockey Federation governs hockey. Other federations cover squash, athletics, wrestling, boxing, football, billiards and snooker, kabaddi, weightlifting and the remaining Olympic sports. Army, navy, WAPDA, railways and departmental clubs have historically supplied a large share of national athletes, especially outside cricket.

Nur Khan’s dual role in hockey and cricket administration is part of the institutional story, as is the long argument about funding, selection and the gap between cricket’s commercial wealth and the rest of the Olympic programme. Titles in this archive are credited to athletes and teams; they were made possible, and sometimes obstructed, by these organisations.
