Home · Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Swat District Headquarters Saidu Sharif · Largest city Mingora · Seven tehsils in the 2023 tables · Population 2,687,384 Swat is the long green valley of the Swat River, from the hot fields near Barikot up to the snow line at Kalam. People outside KP still call it the Switzerland of Pakistan. People who live here call the lower town Mingora, the old seat Saidu Sharif, and the rest by tehsil: Kabal, Matta, Khwaza Khela, Charbagh, Bahrain. Pakistan Bureau of Statistics 2023 counted 2,687,384 people in 381,212 households. About 794,000 of them are urban; the rest are rural. Literacy is 48.13 percent overall — 61.83 percent for men, 33.95 percent for women. Pashto is the mother tongue of about 2.48 million. Kohistani languages (Torwali, Kalami and related speech of the upper valley) are recorded in the tens of thousands. Area of the old district is 5,337 square kilometres. Swat was a princely state until 1969. In late 2025 the provincial cabinet notified a split: lower tehsils staying as Swat (headquarters Gulkada / Babuzai) and upper tehsils as Bar Swat (headquarters Matta). This page still follows the 2023 seven-tehsil census table, because that is the last full PBS count. When the Bureau prints two separate district books, the atlas should split with it. Three largest named places Mingora (Babuzai tehsil) — the bazaar city. Courts, the Mingora Bench of the High Court, the bus yards and most private hospitals sit here. Saidu Sharif is next door: museums, the old palace area, and the name used as headquarters on many official papers. Kabal — municipal council figures in recent compilations sit above one hundred thousand. Kanju airport is on this side of the river. Matta — the main town of the east bank and the notified headquarters of Bar Swat. Second-heaviest tehsil after Babuzai in the 2023 table (552,431 people in the whole tehsil). Bahrain / Madyan and Khwaza Khela are the next names you hear on a bus going up-valley. Seven tehsils (PBS 2023) Babuzai — 297 km², 696,697 people, literacy about 56 percent. Mingora, Saidu Sharif, Gulkada. Matta — 684 km², 552,431 people, literacy about 42.5 percent. Kabal — 485 km², 480,827 people, literacy about 49.3 percent. Khwaza Khela — 392 km², 307,300 people, literacy about 42.6 percent. Bahrain (Behrain) — 2,899 km², 270,623 people, literacy about 39.3 percent. Biggest tehsil by land, thinnest by density. Kalam sits in this tehsil. Barikot — 419 km², 220,148 people, literacy about 50.9 percent. Southern gate toward Malakand and Buner. Charbagh — 161 km², 159,358 people, literacy about 50 percent. Smallest tehsil. Local government papers speak of about 65 union councils (56 rural, 9 urban) and, under the later village-council system, about 170 village councils and 44 neighbourhood councils. That is why nobody can honestly paste “all villages of Swat” on one screen. What follows are names that already sit on council lists, maps and school papers. Bahrain / upper Swat Bahrain, Madyan, Kalam, Utror, Gabral, Mankyal, Kedam, Chail, Bashigram, Matiltan, Balakot (Swat), Ayeen, Aryane, Peshmal side settlements on the Kalam road. This is Swat Kohistan. Winter closes tracks that Mingora never sees. Other names that appear on public photo and map lists: Malam Jabba, Fizagat / Fiza Gut on the river, Marghazar above Saidu, and the Buddhist sites at Butkara and Shingardar near the lower towns. Spellings jump (Bahrain / Behrain, Khwaza Khela / Khawaza Khela, Kuza Bandai / Koza Bandai). Both forms are the same place. Schools and hospitals University of Swat, the women’s campus, Swat Medical College, University of Agriculture Swat, and Government Postgraduate Jahanzeb College are the names students actually use. Government degree colleges sit in Saidu Sharif and the tehsil towns. The female literacy number — just under 34 percent — is the line that should stay on this page even when the tourism paragraphs get longer. Saidu Group of Hospitals / the teaching cluster in Saidu Sharif is the main public referral. Private hospitals crowd Mingora. From Kalam or Peochar an ambulance still measures the trip in hours, not minutes. Work and the river Lower Swat farms wheat, maize, orchards and off-season vegetables. Upper Swat sells timber, potatoes and the tourist season. Hotels from Fizagat to Kalam are a winter-and-summer economy. After the flood years, many of those rooms had to be rebuilt. The river is the reason people come and the reason roads wash out. Kanju (Saidu Sharif) airport is a short drive from Mingora. When flights run, they change the weekend crowd. When they do not, the GT-style road through Malakand is the only gate most families use. What this page will not do It will not list 170 village councils as if they were 170 complete household registers. It will not pretend the 2025 split already has two finished PBS books. It will not write tourism slogans in place of the literacy table. Add a kally name when you live there. That is how Buner was written and how Swat should grow.
