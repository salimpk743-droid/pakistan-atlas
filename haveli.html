Haveli

A dramatic mountain district of deep forests, high passes, remote valleys and peaceful Himalayan landscapes — where the mountains of southern Kashmir rise toward the Pir Panjal.

Haveli District • Azad Jammu & Kashmir • Pakistan

Region Azad Jammu & Kashmir
District HQ Forward Kahuta
Area approximately 598 km²

Haveli District lies in the eastern part of Azad Jammu & Kashmir and is one of the territory's most mountainous landscapes. The district forms part of the Lesser Himalayan Zone and is characterized by steep mountain slopes, deep valleys, forests, streams and high-altitude passes.

Among its best-known natural and scenic destinations are Forward Kahuta, Lasdana, Haji Pir, Bedori, Neelfairy, Kalamula, Sankh Meadows and the numerous waterfalls and mountain valleys scattered throughout the district.

“Haveli is where Kashmir becomes wild and mountainous — deep forests below, high passes above and quiet valleys stretching between the Pir Panjal hills.”

The Extraordinary Beauty of Haveli

Haveli is not defined by one famous attraction. Its character comes from an entire landscape of forests, mountain passes, high ridges, waterfalls, meadows and remote valleys.

The district is especially remarkable because its elevation changes dramatically across relatively short distances. Lower valleys rise toward heavily forested slopes and eventually toward some of the highest mountain terrain in southern Azad Jammu & Kashmir.

Mountain Kashmir

Where Deep Forests Meet the High Pir Panjal

Haveli occupies a distinctive position within Azad Jammu & Kashmir. It is not simply a valley district and not entirely an alpine wilderness. Instead, its landscape rises through layers of villages, cultivated slopes, dense forests, mountain ridges and high passes.

Government geological information places Haveli within the Lesser Himalayan Zone, with elevations ranging from approximately 520 metres to around 3,800 metres. The Pir Panjal forms the dominant mountain system and gives the district much of its dramatic character.

This vertical variation creates remarkable scenery. A journey through Haveli can move from settled valleys into thick coniferous forests and eventually toward cold, high mountain country.

The result is a landscape that feels remote, green and intensely mountainous — especially during the warmer months.

Forward Kahuta — The Mountain Heart of Haveli

Forward Kahuta is the headquarters and principal urban centre of Haveli District.

The settlement lies within a mountainous environment of steep slopes, forested hills, streams and deep valleys. It serves as the administrative, commercial and social centre for communities spread throughout the district.

The wider Forward Kahuta area contains some of the more lightly developed forest landscapes of AJK. Blue pine, deodar and oak are among the important trees found within the mountain environment.

Its relatively remote character has helped preserve a quieter mountain atmosphere. Visitors interested in forests, mountain roads, birdwatching and nature photography can find considerable appeal here.

Travel in this region requires planning because roads and weather conditions can change quickly, particularly during winter.

Lasdana — Forests, Heights & Mountain Silence

Lasdana is one of Haveli's best-known scenic mountain destinations.

The landscape around Lasdana is dominated by forests, mountain slopes and elevated viewpoints. The combination of altitude and vegetation creates a cool, peaceful environment during much of the year.

The approach to Lasdana is itself part of the experience. Mountain roads wind through forested terrain, revealing changing views of ridges and valleys as the elevation increases.

For photographers, Lasdana offers the kind of scenery that changes with weather and light: misty forests, distant ridges, cloud-covered mountains and clear summer horizons.

Haji Pir — The High Mountain Pass

Haji Pir is one of the most prominent geographical landmarks associated with Haveli and the wider Poonch region.

The pass lies within the Pir Panjal mountain system and reaches an elevation of approximately 3,421 metres. Its high elevation makes it one of the most dramatic mountain landscapes in southern AJK.

Historically, mountain passes such as Haji Pir were important corridors through the rugged terrain of Kashmir and Poonch. They connected valleys and communities across difficult mountain country.

Today, the surrounding landscape remains notable for steep ridges, forests, open highland terrain and severe winter conditions.

Snow can make access difficult during winter, and visitors should always check local road and weather conditions before travelling toward the pass.

Bedori — Haveli's High Mountain Landscape

Bedori represents the high-altitude character of Haveli.

The mountain landscape around Bedori is defined by steep slopes, high ridges, changing vegetation and broad views across the surrounding Himalayan terrain.

The area is especially attractive to visitors interested in hiking, mountain photography and highland scenery.

As with other high mountain destinations in Haveli, weather conditions can change rapidly and winter access may become difficult.

Neelfairy — A Quiet Mountain Landscape

Neelfairy, also written as Neilferi in some references, is one of the scenic destinations associated with the Kahuta area of Haveli.

The area combines forested mountains, streams and peaceful surroundings. It represents the quieter side of Haveli tourism, away from large urban centres and heavily developed resorts.

Neelfairy is particularly attractive for landscape photography, nature walks and visitors who want to experience the natural atmosphere of the Himalayan foothills.

The Waterfalls of Haveli

Haveli's steep mountain terrain naturally creates numerous streams, cascades and waterfalls.

Seasonal rainfall and snowmelt feed the mountain watercourses, creating stronger flows during wetter periods.

Among the waterfalls associated with Haveli are Hillan Waterfall, the waterfall areas around Kalamula and other smaller cascades found throughout the district's valleys.

These waterfalls are particularly attractive because they are often surrounded by forests and relatively undeveloped mountain landscapes.

Kalamula — Valleys, Streams & Waterfalls

Kalamula is a scenic locality surrounded by the characteristic mountains and valleys of Haveli.

The area is associated with streams and waterfalls and provides another example of the district's natural variety.

The journey through rural Kalamula also reveals the human side of the mountains — small settlements, agricultural land, livestock and homes scattered among the hills.

Sankh Meadows — Open Mountain Country

The higher areas of Haveli open into mountain meadows where the dense forests give way to broader views and open highland terrain.

Sankh Meadows represents this quieter side of the district's mountain landscape.

During warmer months, high meadows become greener and more inviting, providing opportunities for hiking, photography and peaceful outdoor exploration.

The Forests of Haveli

If the high passes are Haveli's dramatic face, its forests are the soul of the district.

Much of Haveli's mountain slopes are covered with coniferous forests. Blue pine, deodar and oak form important components of the local forest environment.

These forests transform the character of the landscape. Roads disappear into green corridors, slopes become layered with trees and streams move through shaded valleys.

Beyond their beauty, these forests are essential to the mountain ecosystem. They help stabilize slopes, protect watersheds, regulate water flow and provide habitat for wildlife and birds.

Climate & Landscape

A District of Dramatic Elevation

One of Haveli's defining characteristics is its enormous variation in elevation.

Government geological information places the district between approximately 520 metres and 3,800 metres above sea level. Haji Pir rises to about 3,421 metres, while the highest mountain areas approach the upper end of the district's elevation range.

This vertical progression explains much of Haveli's visual diversity. Lower areas contain warmer valleys and settlements, while higher terrain becomes increasingly cold, forested and mountainous.

Winter can be severe in the highlands. Snow may cover mountain passes and affect road access, while summer provides the most comfortable conditions for exploring higher destinations.

Haveli in the History of Poonch & Kashmir

Haveli forms part of the historically important Poonch region, a mountain landscape that has long been connected by passes and routes crossing the valleys of Kashmir.

The rugged geography shaped the history of the area. Mountain passes were not simply geographical features; they were routes through which people, communities and political powers moved across the region.

The wider Poonch region experienced major political changes during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, eventually becoming divided between different administrations after the events surrounding 1947.

Haveli subsequently became part of Azad Jammu & Kashmir and remained administratively associated with Bagh for many years.

Haveli was later established as a separate district, giving the mountainous region its own district-level administrative identity.

Haji Pir — A Pass with Historical Importance

Haji Pir is important not only because of its elevation but also because of its location within the historical geography of Poonch and Kashmir.

Mountain passes have traditionally served as natural corridors through difficult terrain. Haji Pir's position within the Pir Panjal made it part of a landscape where movement between valleys and mountain communities depended heavily on high routes.

The pass later became associated with the military history of the Kashmir region during the conflicts of the twentieth century.

Today, Haji Pir remains both a powerful natural landmark and a reminder of the strategic importance of the mountains.

Rural Life in Haveli

Haveli is fundamentally a mountain district shaped by its villages.

Homes are scattered across slopes and valleys, while small agricultural plots occupy terraces and comparatively level areas.

Livestock, farming, forests and family networks remain important parts of everyday life.

The landscape therefore represents more than wilderness. It is a living mountain environment shaped by generations of communities adapting to difficult terrain and changing seasons.

Culture & Mountain Communities

Haveli belongs to the cultural landscape of Poonch and southern Azad Jammu & Kashmir.

Local life reflects strong family relationships, village traditions, agriculture, religious practices and the rhythms of mountain life.

Pahari and Gujari are important languages of the district, while Kashmiri and Urdu are also used in different communities and official contexts.

Haveli for Photography

Haveli is an exceptional landscape photography destination because its scenery changes dramatically with elevation, weather and light.

Forward Kahuta provides forested mountain scenes. Lasdana offers elevated landscapes. Haji Pir creates a dramatic high-pass environment. Bedori opens toward high mountain scenery, while Neelfairy and Kalamula provide quieter forest and valley perspectives.

Morning mist, clouds moving across the ridges and sunlight filtering through coniferous forests can completely transform the same landscape from one hour to the next.

A District of Quiet Mountain Tourism

Haveli does not have the same level of tourism development as some of the larger destinations of northern Pakistan.

That is part of its appeal.

The district offers forests without large crowds, mountain roads without constant commercial development and valleys where traditional rural life remains visible.

Visitors should not expect extensive infrastructure in remote areas. Instead, Haveli rewards travellers who come prepared, travel respectfully and appreciate the landscape itself.

“Haveli is where the mountains become quieter — forests below, high passes above and hidden valleys between them.”

From the forested slopes around Forward Kahuta to the high landscape of Haji Pir and Bedori, Haveli offers a journey through one of the less explored mountain environments of Azad Jammu & Kashmir.

Its greatest attraction is not a single monument or resort. It is the feeling of entering a landscape where forests, mountains, streams and villages still exist together as one continuous environment.

Haveli Through the Seasons

Spring — The Mountains Turn Green

Spring brings fresh growth to forests and mountain slopes. Streams become lively and the landscape begins to regain its deep green character.

Summer — The Mountain Escape

Summer is one of the most attractive periods for exploring the higher parts of Haveli. Temperatures are generally more comfortable in the mountains than in the plains.

Monsoon — Waterfalls & Deep Green Forests

Rain intensifies the colour of forests and increases the flow of streams and waterfalls. However, heavy rain can also affect mountain roads and create difficult travel conditions.

Autumn — Clear Mountain Landscapes

Autumn brings cooler air and often provides beautiful conditions for photography and long mountain journeys.

Winter — The High Country Changes

Higher areas can become extremely cold and snowfall may affect mountain passes and roads. Conditions should always be checked before travelling toward Haji Pir, Bedori or other high destinations.

Protecting Haveli's Natural Beauty

Haveli's greatest attraction is its natural environment. Forests, mountain streams, meadows and highland ecosystems require responsible tourism.

  • Never leave plastic or other waste in forests or meadows.
  • Do not damage deodar, pine, oak or other forest vegetation.
  • Avoid disturbing wildlife and natural habitats.
  • Respect local villages, homes and private property.
  • Do not pollute streams, springs or waterfalls.
  • Stay on appropriate roads and trekking routes.
  • Check weather and road conditions before entering high mountains.
  • Carry identification and follow local security instructions.
  • Support local guides, accommodation and small businesses.

Why Haveli Is One of Kashmir's Most Beautiful Mountain Districts

Haveli is beautiful because it combines several different mountain environments within one district.

There are the deep forests around Forward Kahuta, the elevated landscapes of Lasdana, the dramatic heights of Haji Pir, the high mountain scenery of Bedori, the peaceful forests of Neelfairy and the waterfalls and meadows scattered throughout the valleys.

Together they create a landscape that feels deeply connected with the mountains of southern Kashmir: green, remote, forested and peaceful.

The Pir Panjal gives Haveli its dramatic skyline, while its forests and valleys provide the softer beauty beneath it.

Haveli therefore deserves a special place on the tourism map of Azad Jammu & Kashmir: it is a district of forests, high passes, waterfalls, mountain villages, history and quiet natural beauty.

حویلی — پونچھ اور کشمیر کی تاریخ میں

یہاں کا دشوار گزار جغرافیہ خود خطے کی تاریخ کا اہم حصہ رہا ہے۔ پہاڑی درے اور راستے مختلف آبادیوں اور وادیوں کے درمیان رابطے کا ذریعہ بنتے رہے۔

انیسویں اور بیسویں صدی کے دوران وسیع تر پونچھ خطے میں کئی اہم سیاسی تبدیلیاں آئیں اور 1947ء کے واقعات کے بعد علاقہ مختلف انتظامی حصوں میں تقسیم ہوا۔

حویلی بعد ازاں آزاد جموں و کشمیر کا حصہ بن گیا اور کئی سال تک انتظامی طور پر ضلع باغ سے منسلک رہا۔

بعد میں حویلی کو الگ ضلع کا درجہ دیا گیا اور اس خطے کو اپنی ضلعی انتظامی شناخت حاصل ہوئی۔

حاجی پیر — تاریخ کا ایک بلند درہ

حاجی پیر کی اہمیت صرف اس کی بلندی کی وجہ سے نہیں بلکہ اس کی تاریخی جغرافیائی حیثیت کی وجہ سے بھی ہے۔

پہاڑی درے ہمیشہ سے مشکل پہاڑی علاقوں میں سفر کے قدرتی راستے رہے ہیں۔ حاجی پیر بھی پونچھ اور کشمیر کے پہاڑی علاقوں میں رابطے کے ایسے راستوں کا حصہ رہا ہے۔

بیسویں صدی کے کشمیر کے تنازعات کے دوران بھی حاجی پیر کی جغرافیائی اور عسکری اہمیت نمایاں رہی۔

آج یہ مقام ایک شاندار قدرتی پہاڑی منظر اور خطے کی تاریخی اہمیت دونوں کی یاد دلاتا ہے۔

حویلی کی دیہی زندگی

حویلی کشمیر کے خوبصورت پہاڑی اضلاع میں کیوں خاص ہے؟

حویلی اس لیے منفرد ہے کہ ایک ہی ضلع میں مختلف نوعیت کے پہاڑی ماحول ایک ساتھ موجود ہیں۔

فارورڈ کہوٹہ کے گھنے جنگلات، لسڈنہ کے بلند مناظر، حاجی پیر کی ڈرامائی بلندی، بیڈوری کے پہاڑ، نیلفیری کے پرسکون جنگلات اور کلامولہ کی آبشاریں حویلی کو ایک متنوع قدرتی شناخت دیتے ہیں۔

یہ سب مل کر جنوبی کشمیر کے ایسے پہاڑی منظر کو تشکیل دیتے ہیں جو سبز، پرسکون، نسبتاً دور افتادہ اور قدرتی محسوس ہوتا ہے۔

پیر پنجال حویلی کو ایک شاندار پہاڑی افق دیتا ہے جبکہ گھنے جنگلات اور وادیاں اس کے نیچے ایک نرم اور گہرا سبز منظر پیدا کرتی ہیں۔

اسی لیے حویلی آزاد جموں و کشمیر کے سیاحتی نقشے پر ایک خاص مقام رکھتا ہے — یہ جنگلات، بلند دروں، آبشاروں، پہاڑی بستیوں، تاریخ اور خاموش قدرتی حسن کا ضلع ہے۔
