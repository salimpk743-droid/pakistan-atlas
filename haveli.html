Azad Jammu & Kashmir

Haveli

A remote mountain district where deep forests, high passes, waterfalls and traditional Himalayan valleys come together along the western edge of Kashmir.

From the forested slopes around Forward Kahuta to the high landscapes of Haji Pir, Lasdana and Bedori, Haveli is one of the lesser-known mountain regions of Azad Jammu & Kashmir.

Headquarters Forward Kahuta
Region Poonch Division
Area 598 km²
Population 152,124 · 2017
Mountain Kashmir

Haveli: Where forests climb toward the high passes

Haveli is a district of steep mountains, narrow valleys, forested slopes, high meadows and settlements scattered across the landscape. Its headquarters, Forward Kahuta, sits within a mountainous environment associated with clear streams and Western Himalayan forests.

The district is especially distinctive because of the way its landscapes change with elevation. Lower valleys support villages and agriculture, while higher areas open into coniferous forests, mountain pastures and exposed ridges.

Geography

Haveli: A landscape shaped by mountains

Steep valleys and ridges

Haveli lies within a strongly mountainous landscape. Valleys are separated by ridges and steep slopes, producing a constantly changing pattern of forests, cultivated land and small settlements.

Roads frequently follow valleys, streams and mountain contours, while routes toward higher passes become more demanding as elevation increases.

The Western Himalayas

The forests around Forward Kahuta include Western Himalayan moist-temperate vegetation, with blue pine, deodar and oak among the characteristic trees.

The combination of forest, altitude and rainfall gives Haveli a distinctly green character during much of the warmer season.

District headquarters

Forward Kahuta

Forward Kahuta is the administrative headquarters of Haveli and one of the principal settlements in this remote mountain region.

The surrounding terrain is steep and heavily forested, with valleys cut by streams and small settlements spread across the hillsides.

The wider Forward Kahuta area is particularly attractive for travellers interested in forest landscapes rather than heavily developed resort environments.

06
Water and valleys

Haveli: Streams running through the mountains

Water is one of the defining elements of Haveli's geography. Mountain streams and nullahs cut through valleys and contribute to the green appearance of the district.

The district's drainage is connected with the wider Poonch river system, with streams such as Betar and Palangi forming important local watercourses.

During wetter periods, waterfalls become particularly noticeable, while in winter higher elevations can experience snow and frozen mountain conditions.

07
High country

Haveli: From forest valleys to the high ridges

Haji Pir landscape

The Haji Pir area represents the high-pass character of Haveli. Roads and tracks climb away from the valley settlements toward exposed mountain country.

At these elevations, weather can change rapidly, and winter conditions can make travel difficult.

Bedori country

The higher ridges around Bedori give Haveli some of its most dramatic mountain scenery.

The landscape becomes increasingly open as the forests give way to high slopes, ridgelines and mountain-top terrain.

Climate

Haveli: A mountain climate that changes with altitude

Spring Green slopes, fresh vegetation and increasingly pleasant mountain weather.
Summer Cooler mountain conditions than the lowlands, with June–September often favourable for travel.
Autumn Clearer mountain views and changing forest colours create a quieter landscape.
Winter Cold conditions and snowfall become more important at higher elevations.
09
History

Haveli: A frontier mountain district

Earlier period

Haveli as a mountain sub-region

The area historically formed part of the wider Poonch–Bagh mountain region and developed through scattered settlements, agricultural communities and routes crossing the mountains.

1987

Bagh becomes a separate district

Haveli remained administratively connected with the changing district structure of the surrounding Poonch and Bagh region.

2009

Haveli becomes a district

Haveli was established as a separate district, with Forward Kahuta serving as its headquarters.

Today

A growing tourism identity

The district is increasingly recognised for its highland scenery, forests, waterfalls, passes and relatively undeveloped mountain environment.

Everyday life

Haveli: Life between fields, forests and mountains

Rural Haveli is shaped by geography. Homes and villages often occupy terraces, plateaus or valley edges where land can be cultivated and access to water is possible.

Livestock, small-scale agriculture, local trade and employment outside the district all contribute to household life.

The mountainous environment also creates a strong relationship between communities and forests, springs, grazing areas and seasonal routes.

Travel

Haveli: The journey is part of the experience

Mountain roads

Access through Haveli involves steep terrain, winding roads and mountain approaches.

Conditions can vary significantly with weather, particularly in winter and during heavy rainfall.

Travel prepared

  • Check road conditions before travelling.
  • Carry identification in sensitive border areas.
  • Allow extra time for mountain roads.
  • Carry water and basic supplies.
  • Avoid isolated routes after dark.
14
Conservation

Haveli: Protecting a fragile mountain environment

Haveli's greatest tourism asset is its natural landscape. The same forests, streams and highland environments that attract visitors are also sensitive to uncontrolled development and waste.

Responsible tourism should therefore focus on keeping valleys clean, protecting forest vegetation, avoiding damage to streams and respecting local communities.

Visitors can help simply by carrying their waste back, avoiding unnecessary disturbance to wildlife and choosing local services where possible.

Haveli District: FAQ

What is the population of Haveli District?

The 2017 census counted 146,220 people. The AJ&K Bureau of Statistics projects about 0.159 million for 2023. (AJ&K Bureau of Statistics)

How many tehsils are in Haveli District?

The 2017 census tables list 2: Haveli/Mumtazabad and Khurshidabad. (AJ&K Bureau of Statistics)

Which division is Haveli District in?

Poonch Division. AJK has three divisions (Muzaffarabad, Poonch and Mirpur) and 10 districts. (AJ&K Bureau of Statistics)
