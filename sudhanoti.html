Azad Jammu & Kashmir

Sudhanoti

A green mountain district where Pallandri's hilltop landscape, forested ridges, historic sites and traditional Pahari communities define the character of southern Azad Jammu & Kashmir.

From the historic surroundings of Pallandri to the highlands of Trarkhel and Baloch, Sudhanoti combines mountain scenery with a deep historical and cultural identity.

Mountain Kashmir

Where hill towns meet green mountain country

Sudhanoti is one of the districts of Azad Jammu & Kashmir and lies in the south-western part of the territory. Its landscape is characterised by hills, forests, valleys, cultivated slopes and small mountain settlements.

Pallandri, the district headquarters, sits high above the Mahl River valley and acts as the administrative, educational and commercial centre of the district.

Geography

A district of hills, valleys and forests

The western gateway

Sudhanoti occupies the south-western part of Azad Jammu & Kashmir. The district is bordered by Poonch to the north and east, Kotli to the south and Punjab to the west.

Its position makes it an important mountain connection between the AJK hill districts and the Punjab side of the region.

Green mountain country

Forested slopes, terraced farmland and ridges surround many settlements. Pine forests are especially visible around higher areas.

The district receives substantial rainfall and is recognised as one of the greener areas of southern AJK.

03
District headquarters

Pallandri

Pallandri is the headquarters and principal town of Sudhanoti. It stands on a ridge above the Mahl River valley at an elevation of roughly 1,400 metres.

The town functions as an administrative and market centre, with traditional bazaars, educational institutions, government offices and services for surrounding mountain communities.

Forests and terraced agricultural land surround the town, while walking routes and viewpoints lead away from the built-up area into the surrounding hills.

Highlands

Trarkhel and the upper mountain country

A cool highland environment

Trarkhel stands at approximately 1,982 metres, making it one of the more elevated settlements in the district.

Its altitude gives it a cooler mountain climate and broad views across the surrounding landscape.

A gateway to the hills

Trarkhel is a useful stop for travellers exploring the higher parts of Sudhanoti and the wider Poonch mountain region.

The surrounding landscape combines settlements, forests, roads and open mountain slopes.

07
Southern Sudhanoti

Baloch and the higher forests

Baloch lies in the southern part of Sudhanoti and represents another side of the district's mountain geography.

Higher slopes around the area provide views toward the Pir Panjal landscape, while forests and winter snowfall add to its highland character.

Local villages and shrines are part of the cultural landscape, giving the area significance beyond its scenery.

08
Natural attraction

Tattapani — the hot springs of the Poonch River

Warm mineral waters

Tattapani is famous for its naturally warm sulphur-rich springs beside the Poonch River.

The springs have long attracted visitors, particularly during the colder months.

River landscape

The hot springs sit within a river valley, creating a striking contrast between warm mineral water and the surrounding mountain environment.

Tattapani is one of the best-known natural attractions associated with the wider Sudhanoti–Poonch region.

09
History

A district with a deep political history

19th century

Sudhanoti as a historic tehsil

Sudhanoti was established as a tehsil within the wider Poonch jagir during the nineteenth century.

Sikh & Dogra period

Conflict and resistance

The region experienced political upheaval during the Sikh and later Dogra periods. The Suddhan tribes played a significant role in local resistance movements.

1837–1839

Baral Fort

Baral Fort was constructed during the Dogra period near Pallandri as part of the military and administrative infrastructure of the time.

1947

The Kashmir conflict and Azad Kashmir

Sudhanoti became closely associated with the political and military events surrounding the formation of Azad Kashmir.

Early Azad Kashmir

Chonjal Hill and the early government

Chonjal Hill near Pallandri holds an important place in the early political history of Azad Kashmir.

11
People and culture

The Sudhan heartland

Sudhanoti takes its name from the Sudhan people, who form the dominant population group of the district.

The culture of the region reflects the wider Pahari mountain environment of AJK, with strong traditions of hospitality, family networks, village life, agriculture and military service.

Pahari is an important spoken language, while Gujari, Kashmiri and Urdu are also used across different communities and contexts.

14
Public services

Healthcare and district services

Pallandri serves as the district's main administrative centre and hosts important public services.

District-level healthcare services are centred around the district headquarters, while rural health facilities serve communities farther into the mountain areas.

Because of the district's mountainous geography, travel time between remote villages and major service centres can be an important practical consideration.

Getting there

The mountain road to Sudhanoti

From Rawalpindi and Islamabad

Pallandri is connected with Rawalpindi and Islamabad through the Azad Pattan route.

The journey transitions from the plains into increasingly mountainous terrain.

Mountain travel

  • Check weather before travelling.
  • Allow extra time on mountain roads.
  • Keep basic supplies for remote routes.
  • Follow local travel guidance.
  • Take additional care during winter.
18
Conservation

Keeping the hills green

Sudhanoti's forests and mountain landscapes are among its most valuable natural resources.

Responsible tourism means keeping forests and viewpoints free of litter, protecting streams, respecting wildlife and avoiding unnecessary damage to vegetation.

Local communities should remain central to tourism development so that visitors contribute to the district without eroding its traditional character.
